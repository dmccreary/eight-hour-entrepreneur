    Quiz: Revenue Models & Pricing¶

    Test your understanding of choosing, validating, and pricing a revenue model with these review questions.

    1. How does this chapter define a revenue model?¶

    1. The specific structure by which a price is set and charged to a customer
    2. A distinct source of income within a venture, such as a specific offer or customer segment
    3. The reasoning behind why a particular pricing model was chosen
    4. The overall structure describing how a venture generates income from its offer — the pattern by which money flows in at all, not a specific dollar amount
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A revenue model is the overall structure describing how a venture generates income from its offer, not a specific dollar amount but the pattern by which money flows in at all. Option A describes a pricing model, option B describes a revenue stream, and option C describes a pricing strategy — all related but distinct terms this chapter separates carefully.

    2. How does this chapter distinguish a pricing model from a revenue model?¶

    1. A pricing model and a revenue model are the same structure described at two different points in the venture's life
    2. A pricing model is the specific structure by which a price is set and charged, distinct from the revenue model's broader income pattern
    3. A pricing model only applies to recurring revenue, while a revenue model only applies to transaction revenue
    4. A pricing model is chosen before a revenue stream exists, while a revenue model is chosen after
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A pricing model is the specific structure by which a price is set and charged to the customer, distinct from the revenue model's broader income pattern. A founder first settles on a revenue stream's shape, then chooses a pricing model to actually implement it.

    3. Jordan chooses value-based pricing anchored to what customers said driving to a groomer already cost them in lost time, and ties that choice back to his target customer and value proposition. What is Jordan applying?¶

    1. A revenue projection
    2. Break-even thinking
    3. A pricing strategy — the reasoning behind why a particular pricing model was chosen, tied to the target customer, value proposition, and competitive landscape
    4. Pricing validation
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A pricing strategy is the reasoning behind why a particular pricing model was chosen, tied back to the target customer, the value proposition, and the competitive landscape from earlier chapters. Jordan tying his price to what customers said their lost time was worth is exactly that kind of reasoning.

    4. A customer pays a fixed amount every month for ongoing access to a service, with no separate charge per visit. Which pricing model is this?¶

    1. Subscription pricing
    2. One-time pricing
    3. Tiered pricing
    4. Cost-plus pricing
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Subscription pricing charges the customer a fixed amount on a recurring schedule in exchange for ongoing access. Billing the same fixed amount every month with no per-visit charge is exactly this structure, best suited to an ongoing need and recurring revenue.

    5. How does one-time pricing differ from subscription pricing, according to this chapter's table?¶

    1. One-time pricing is only used for digital offers, while subscription pricing is only used for physical ones
    2. One-time pricing charges customers monthly, while subscription pricing charges customers per transaction
    3. One-time pricing requires a freemium tier, while subscription pricing does not
    4. One-time pricing charges a single amount for a single transaction with no ongoing commitment, while subscription pricing charges a fixed amount on a recurring schedule
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. One-time pricing charges the customer a single amount for a single transaction, with no ongoing commitment on either side — best fit for occasional needs and transaction revenue. Subscription pricing instead charges a fixed, recurring amount for ongoing access.

    6. A venture offers a basic package, a standard package, and a premium package, each with more features at a higher price, letting customers pick the level that fits their needs and budget. Which pricing model is this?¶

    1. Tiered pricing
    2. Freemium model
    3. Cost-plus pricing
    4. Subscription pricing
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Tiered pricing offers the same core solution at multiple price and feature levels, letting customers self-select based on their own needs and budget. A freemium model is related but specifically involves a free basic tier, which this scenario doesn't describe.

    7. Jordan's $40 driveway price emerged from anchoring to what customers said driving to a groomer already cost them in lost time. Which pricing approach does this reflect, and what does it deliberately avoid?¶

    1. Cost-plus pricing, which avoids setting a price the customer is willing to pay
    2. Tiered pricing, which avoids offering only one price level
    3. Freemium pricing, which avoids charging for the basic tier
    4. Value-based pricing, which sets the price according to how much value the customer believes they're receiving rather than what the offer costs to deliver
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Value-based pricing sets the price according to how much value the customer believes they're receiving, grounded in the benefit articulated back in Chapter 8, rather than in what the offer costs to deliver. Jordan's price came from value-based thinking, not from totaling supply costs and adding a margin, which is what cost-plus pricing does instead.

    8. What is the key weakness of cost-plus pricing, as this chapter describes it?¶

    1. It cannot be combined with a revenue stream
    2. It guarantees profitability on paper but ignores what the customer would actually be willing to pay
    3. It only works for recurring revenue streams
    4. It requires a validation milestone before it can be used
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Cost-plus pricing sets the price by starting from what the offer costs to deliver and adding a fixed margin on top. That guarantees profitability on paper, but the chapter is explicit that it ignores what the customer would actually be willing to pay — the exact gap value-based pricing is designed to close.

    9. What is a revenue stream?¶

    1. The specific structure by which a price is set and charged to a customer
    2. An unverified belief about what a customer will pay and how often
    3. A distinct source of income within a venture, which may come from different offers, customer segments, or pricing arrangements for the same core offer
    4. The point at which total revenue exactly covers total costs
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A revenue stream is a distinct source of income within a venture, which may come from different offers, different customer segments, or different pricing arrangements for the same core offer. A revenue model is built from one or more of these streams, each shaped as either recurring or transaction revenue.

    10. A venture designs a monthly grooming plan where the customer is billed every month for ongoing service. Which revenue shape does this represent?¶

    1. Transaction revenue
    2. Revenue projection
    3. Recurring revenue
    4. Revenue assumption
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Recurring revenue is income collected repeatedly from the same customer on an ongoing basis, like a subscription renewing every month. The chapter contrasts this directly with Jordan's actual $40-per-session model, which is transaction revenue rather than recurring revenue.

    11. Jordan's $40-per-session model charges customers once per visit with no ongoing commitment. A hypothetical monthly grooming plan, by contrast, would bill customers every month. What does comparing these two reveal about transaction revenue?¶

    1. Transaction revenue is income collected once per discrete purchase with no ongoing commitment, unlike recurring revenue's repeated, ongoing collection
    2. Transaction revenue and recurring revenue are the same shape applied to different pricing models
    3. Transaction revenue only applies to subscription pricing, never to one-time pricing
    4. Transaction revenue requires a freemium tier to function
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Transaction revenue is income collected once per discrete purchase, with no ongoing commitment from the customer. Jordan's $40-per-session model is the chapter's own example, explicitly contrasted with a hypothetical monthly plan that would instead be recurring revenue.

    12. A founder has set a price and is now looking at real transaction data rather than opinions to confirm it produces enough paying customers to sustain the venture. Which activity is this founder performing?¶

    1. Price point testing, since only two prices are being compared
    2. Pricing validation — confirming, through real transactions rather than opinions, that a chosen price point produces enough paying customers to sustain the venture
    3. Break-even thinking, since the founder is calculating fixed costs
    4. Revenue projection, since the founder is estimating future income
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Pricing validation is confirming, through real transactions rather than opinions, that a chosen price point produces enough paying customers to sustain the venture. It comes after price point testing has already compared different prices, so this scenario — confirming one already-chosen price — is pricing validation specifically.

    13. How does this chapter define willingness to pay?¶

    1. An unverified belief about what a customer will pay and how often
    2. The rate at which people who show interest actually become paying customers
    3. The point at which total revenue exactly covers total costs
    4. The actual maximum amount a specific customer would pay for a specific offer, discoverable only through real behavior, not a survey question
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Willingness to pay is the actual maximum amount a specific customer would pay for a specific offer, which can only be discovered through real behavior, not guessed from a survey question. Option A instead describes a revenue assumption, and option B describes sales conversion.

    14. A founder offers $35 to one group of prospective customers and $45 to another group at the same time, then compares how many say yes at each price. Which practice is this founder using?¶

    1. Price point testing
    2. Pricing validation
    3. Break-even thinking
    4. Sales conversion
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Price point testing is the lean test, following the same principles from Chapter 10, of offering the same or similar offer at different prices to different customers to observe how willingness to pay actually changes. Offering $35 and $45 to two groups at the same time is the chapter's own suggested version of this test.

    15. One founder estimates next quarter's income using actual price points and actual test results from Chapter 10. A second founder estimates next quarter's income from an optimistic guess about how sales might go. Which founder is producing a genuine revenue projection, as this chapter defines the term?¶

    1. The second founder, since a revenue projection should always assume the best case
    2. Neither founder, since a revenue projection requires break-even thinking first
    3. The first founder, since a revenue projection is an estimate of future income built from real evidence rather than optimistic guessing
    4. Both founders equally, since any income estimate counts as a revenue projection
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A revenue projection is an estimate of future income, built from real evidence — actual price points, actual test results — rather than optimistic guessing. The chapter's own warning is direct: a revenue projection built on real evidence is worth far more than an optimistic guess, even a modest one.

    16. A founder wants to build a revenue projection and has two options: use the actual rate at which interested people became paying customers in a Chapter 10 field test, or assume a conversion rate that "feels about right." Which measurement should the founder use, and what is it called?¶

    1. Willingness to pay, measured from a survey question
    2. Break-even thinking, measured from fixed costs
    3. Pricing validation, measured from a single transaction
    4. Sales conversion, the rate at which people who show interest actually become paying customers, measured directly from field test results
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Sales conversion is the rate at which people who show interest actually become paying customers, measured directly from field test results like the ones from Chapter 10. The chapter's warning is explicit: use the actual measured conversion rate, even if small and humble, rather than an imagined one that feels right.

    17. A founder has never charged a customer but has written down, "Customers will pay $50 per month for this." Judging this belief against the standards this chapter sets for a sound revenue assumption, what is missing?¶

    1. Nothing — a revenue assumption is expected to remain a belief and never gets tested
    2. Field-tested evidence — a revenue assumption is an unverified belief about what a customer will pay, and this one hasn't yet received the same field-tested treatment as every assumption since Chapter 1
    3. A freemium tier, since every revenue assumption must include one
    4. A tiered pricing structure, since flat pricing cannot support a revenue assumption
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A revenue assumption is an unverified belief about what a customer will pay and how often, which needs the same field-tested treatment as every assumption since Chapter 1. Simply writing down a number without testing it leaves the assumption unverified, exactly the gap price point testing and pricing validation are designed to close.

    18. Two founders present the same $40 price to a customer. One states the price plainly with no hidden fees. The other lists a low headline price but adds undisclosed add-on charges at checkout. Judging both against this chapter's standard for pricing communication, which approach is more effective and why?¶

    1. The first founder's approach, because pricing communication should present the price clearly, honestly, and without hidden terms, using the same clarity and simplicity principles as any other customer-facing language
    2. The second founder's approach, because a lower headline price always converts better regardless of hidden fees
    3. Both approaches are equally effective, since pricing communication only concerns the final total, not how it's presented
    4. Neither approach matters, since pricing communication is only relevant after break-even is reached
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Pricing communication is how a price gets presented to the customer — clearly, honestly, and without hidden terms — using the same message clarity and simplicity principles that govern every other piece of customer-facing language from Chapter 12. Hidden add-on charges violate that standard even if the headline price looks more attractive.

    19. Why does this chapter say a freemium model is best suited to digital or software offers rather than most physical, hands-on services?¶

    1. Because physical services always cost more to deliver than digital ones
    2. Because a freemium model offers a free basic tier, and it works best where serving a free user costs the venture very little, which is rarely true of a hands-on service like grooming
    3. Because tiered pricing cannot be combined with a freemium model
    4. Because a freemium model requires recurring revenue and physical services can only use transaction revenue
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A freemium model offers a basic version of a solution for free while charging for a premium tier with additional features — a structure best suited to digital or software offers where serving a free user costs the venture very little. A hands-on service like driveway grooming costs real time and materials per customer, so giving it away free doesn't fit the model the same way.

    20. A founder wants to design a simple monthly framework that tells them, at a glance, exactly how many sales they need before the venture stops losing money each month, using price, cost per sale, and fixed monthly costs. How should the founder construct this framework, based on this chapter's break-even thinking?¶

    1. Track only total revenue each month and ignore fixed costs entirely
    2. Set the price equal to the cost per sale so every sale is automatically profitable
    3. Build the calculation as Fixed Monthly Costs divided by (Price per Sale minus Cost per Sale), recalculating whenever any of the three inputs changes
    4. Estimate break-even from an optimistic sales conversion rate rather than the three cost and price inputs
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Break-even thinking is the discipline of calculating the point at which total revenue exactly covers total costs, built from price, cost per sale, and fixed monthly costs working together. Structuring it as Fixed Monthly Costs divided by (Price per Sale minus Cost per Sale) — recalculated whenever an input changes — is exactly the framework this chapter describes, and it also guards against the case where price never exceeds cost.
