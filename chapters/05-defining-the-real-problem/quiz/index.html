    Quiz: Defining the Real Problem¶

    Test your understanding of defining a validated customer problem with these review questions.

    1. According to Chapter 5, what is a problem definition?¶

    1. The practice of stating, in specific and falsifiable terms, the difficulty a target customer experiences that the venture intends to address
    2. A short slogan used to advertise the eventual solution to potential investors
    3. Any complaint a founder overhears from a stranger, whether or not it relates to the target customer
    4. The founder's best guess at what customers might want, updated only after the business launches
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A problem definition is the practice of stating, in specific and falsifiable terms, the difficulty your target customer experiences that your venture intends to address — specific enough that real evidence could confirm or contradict it. This is the second block on the canvas, and it only makes sense for a specific target customer, not people in general, which is why a vague observation like "people are busy" fails to qualify.

    2. How does the chapter define a pain point?¶

    1. The general negative experience — frustration, cost, wasted time — that results from any unsolved problem
    2. One specific, nameable instance of a customer's pain, concrete enough to describe in a single sentence
    3. The finished sentence that will occupy the Problem block of the canvas
    4. A requirement that no existing option, including doing nothing, adequately satisfies
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A pain point is one specific, nameable instance of a customer's pain, concrete enough to describe in a single sentence — for example, Jordan's customer losing an unpaid afternoon of work to drive forty minutes each way for grooming drop-off. It is narrower than customer pain, which is the general negative experience behind it, and different from a problem statement or an unmet need.

    3. Which statement best distinguishes customer pain from a pain point, as the chapter uses the terms?¶

    1. Customer pain is always financial, while a pain point can be any type of frustration
    2. A pain point is a broad category, while customer pain is one specific instance inside it
    3. Customer pain is the negative experience a customer feels overall, while a pain point is one specific, single-sentence instance of that experience
    4. The two terms mean exactly the same thing and can be used interchangeably in a problem statement
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Customer pain is the negative experience — frustration, cost, wasted time, embarrassment, or risk — a customer feels as a direct result of an unsolved problem. A pain point narrows that broader experience down to one specific, nameable instance concrete enough to state in a single sentence. The two terms describe different levels of specificity, not interchangeable synonyms.

    4. Priya's interviewees kept saying "I don't have time to cook." Why does the chapter treat this as something other than the root cause?¶

    1. Because Priya's interviewees were not part of her validated target customer segment
    2. Because the statement described a product feature rather than a customer experience
    3. Because the complaint was too severe to be believable without further evidence
    4. Because it was a surface-level symptom, and the root cause — schedule unpredictability — lay several layers beneath it
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Priya's interviewees' first complaint, "I don't have time to cook," was a symptom — the surface-level statement offered first. Asking "why" repeatedly revealed the root cause: rotating shift schedules made any planned dinner routine unreliable, not a simple lack of hours. A solution built for the symptom alone would have missed what was actually producing the complaint.

    5. Which sentence best follows the chapter's problem statement pattern of "[Target customer] struggles with [specific pain], caused by [root cause], which matters because [severity/frequency/urgency]"?¶

    1. "Dog grooming should be more convenient for busy pet owners everywhere."
    2. "Jordan wants to build a mobile grooming business that customers will love."
    3. "Dog owners within 5 miles who don't own a car-friendly crate lose a half-day of income every time their dog needs grooming, because the nearest groomer requires in-person drop-off and pickup."
    4. "Most people are too busy to take their dog to the groomer on time."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. This sentence follows the chapter's pattern — target customer, specific pain, root cause, and why it matters — using Jordan's actual refined problem statement: dog owners within 5 miles losing a half-day of income because the nearest groomer requires in-person drop-off and pickup. The other options are vague aspirations or founder-centered statements, not falsifiable problem statements.

    6. What does problem severity measure?¶

    1. How painful or costly the problem is on any single occurrence when it happens
    2. How many times per week the problem tends to recur for the customer
    3. How soon the customer wants the problem solved once it is confirmed real
    4. How many competing alternatives already exist for solving the problem
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Problem severity measures how painful or costly the problem is on any single occurrence — independent of how often it happens or how soon the customer wants it fixed. A flooded basement is an example of high severity paired with low frequency, showing severity is its own distinct dimension from problem frequency and problem urgency.

    7. Priya found her target customers faced their dinner problem 3 to 5 nights a week on a rotating schedule. Which dimension does this finding measure?¶

    1. Problem severity, because it reflects how disruptive each missed dinner is
    2. Problem urgency, because it reflects how soon customers want a fix
    3. Willingness to solve, because it reflects how motivated customers are to pay
    4. Problem frequency, because it reflects how often the problem recurs for the customer
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Problem frequency measures how often the problem recurs for the target customer. Priya's finding that the dinner problem struck 3 to 5 nights per week on a rotating schedule is a frequency measurement, distinct from severity (how painful each occurrence is) and urgency (how soon the customer wants it solved).

    8. A founder finishes a round of interviews with three candidate problems in view. What does the chapter say problem prioritization requires the founder to do?¶

    1. Pursue whichever problem was mentioned first in the very first interview
    2. Rank the candidate problems using severity, frequency, and urgency evidence, then choose which to pursue first
    3. Build a small solution for all three problems simultaneously to see which one sells
    4. Defer the decision until every possible customer has been interviewed at least once
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Problem prioritization is the deliberate act of ranking candidate problems and choosing which one to pursue first, using severity, frequency, and urgency evidence rather than personal preference or the order problems happened to surface. Founders commonly surface more than one candidate problem after a full round of interviews, making this ranking step necessary.

    9. What makes a problem a "real problem" rather than merely a perceived one, according to the chapter?¶

    1. It has been described in a formal problem statement using the canvas's exact wording
    2. It affects a large enough number of people to matter to investors
    3. A founder is personally confident, based on outside research, that it exists
    4. Customers actually experience it and can describe it from their own lived frustration when asked directly, confirmed through evidence
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A real problem is one that customers actually experience and can describe from their own lived frustration when asked directly, confirmed through evidence rather than founder intuition. This distinguishes it from a perceived problem, which a founder believes exists based on logic or outside research but has not yet confirmed with the actual target customer.

    10. How does the chapter define a perceived problem?¶

    1. A problem that has already failed a round of problem validation and should be discarded
    2. A problem invented purely for the purpose of a pitch competition
    3. A problem a founder believes customers have, based on logic, outside research, or personal experience, that hasn't yet been confirmed by talking to the target customer
    4. A problem that only exists in a market survey and has no basis in individual experience
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A perceived problem is one a founder believes customers have, based on logic, outside research, or personal experience, that has not yet been confirmed by talking to the actual target customer. The chapter stresses that every real problem starts out perceived — the mistake is building a solution around it before validation, not holding the perception itself.

    11. Jordan initially assumed, without asking anyone, that dog owners were frustrated by drop-off grooming. What step would the chapter say Jordan needs to take before treating that assumption as a real problem?¶

    1. Problem validation — testing the perceived problem against real customer conversations and behavior
    2. Problem framing — deciding which words to use when describing the problem
    3. Problem prioritization — ranking it against other candidate problems Jordan has noticed
    4. Solution testing — building a small offer and seeing whether anyone buys it
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Problem validation is the process of testing a perceived problem against real customer conversations and behavior to confirm whether it is genuinely felt, exactly as urgently as assumed. Jordan's untested assumption about drop-off frustration remains perceived until real customers confirm it directly — logic and outside research alone cannot make a perceived problem real.

    12. A founder builds a solution that neatly resolves a customer's very first complaint but leaves the deeper, validated cause of that complaint completely untouched. Which concept explains what is missing?¶

    1. Problem framing, because the founder chose the wrong words to describe the problem
    2. Problem-solution fit, because the solution addresses the symptom rather than the validated root cause
    3. Problem urgency, because the founder moved too slowly to help the customer
    4. Essential bills priority, because the customer cannot afford the proposed solution
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Problem-solution fit is the degree to which a proposed solution actually addresses the validated root cause of a problem, as opposed to merely addressing its symptom. A solution that resolves only the first complaint a customer voices, while leaving the deeper cause untouched, has poor problem-solution fit even if it feels responsive in the moment.

    13. In the Five Whys drill-down, Priya's interviewees first said "I don't have time to cook," and only after repeated "why" questions did "rotating shifts make any pre-planned dinner routine unreliable" emerge. What discipline does this drill-down illustrate?¶

    1. Problem prioritization, because it ranks candidate problems by severity and frequency
    2. Problem validation, because it confirms whether a perceived problem is genuinely felt
    3. Symptom vs. root cause, the discipline of not stopping at the first explanation a customer offers
    4. Problem discovery, the ongoing habit of finding and refining problem statements over time
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Symptom vs. root cause is the discipline of not stopping at the first explanation a customer offers, because a solution built for a symptom often leaves the root cause untouched. The repeated "why" technique in Priya's drill-down — sometimes called the Five Whys — is exactly this discipline applied step by step until the answer stops changing.

    14. How does the chapter define an unmet need?¶

    1. A pain point so severe that a customer would pay any price to resolve it
    2. A customer requirement that no current option — including doing nothing — adequately satisfies, whether or not the customer has put it into words
    3. A feature a founder decides to build regardless of customer feedback
    4. The written sentence describing a problem that will sit in the canvas's Problem block
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. An unmet need is a customer requirement that no current option — including doing nothing — adequately satisfies, whether or not the customer has ever put it into words. It is closely related to, but distinct from, a job to be done, which reframes the same territory around the function or outcome the customer is trying to accomplish.

    15. Which framing below correctly expresses Priya's situation as a job to be done, rather than as a product?¶

    1. "Priya should sell a subscription meal-kit service to busy families."
    2. "Priya's customers want a well-known meal-kit brand delivered to their door."
    3. "Priya's customers are frustrated because they don't have time to cook."
    4. "Priya's customers are fundamentally trying to get a nutritious dinner on the table with zero advance planning."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A job to be done reframes the question around function rather than feature: the underlying task or outcome a customer is fundamentally trying to accomplish, independent of any particular product. Framing Priya's customers as wanting "a nutritious dinner on the table with zero advance planning" captures the job, rather than describing a specific product like a meal kit.

    16. Why does the chapter treat problem framing as a meaningful choice rather than a neutral formality?¶

    1. Because the same underlying problem can be worded narrowly or broadly, with very different implications for the Solution block that follows
    2. Because only one legally acceptable wording exists for any given problem statement
    3. Because customers refuse to participate in interviews unless the framing is finalized first
    4. Because framing determines the price a customer will ultimately be willing to pay
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Problem framing is the choice of how to word a problem — which details to include, which customer language to borrow directly, and which angle to emphasize. Because the same underlying problem can be framed narrowly or broadly, that choice carries real implications for how the Solution block gets designed in the next chapter.

    17. What does problem urgency measure, as distinct from severity and frequency?¶

    1. How costly the problem is on any single occurrence when it happens
    2. How often the problem recurs for the target customer
    3. How many direct competitors already address the same problem
    4. How soon the customer wants a solution, independent of severity or frequency
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Problem urgency measures how soon the customer wants a solution, independent of severity or frequency. A customer can feel a problem intensely but rarely, like a flooded basement, or mildly but constantly, like a slightly-too-small kitchen — either combination can still justify a venture, which is why urgency is tracked as its own dimension.

    18. A customer confirms a problem is real, severe, and frequent, but has never spent any money or effort trying to fix it. What does the chapter say a founder should check next?¶

    1. Whether the problem has been framed narrowly enough for a clear problem statement
    2. Whether a direct competitor already offers a solution to the same problem
    3. Willingness to solve — whether the customer is actually motivated to spend money, time, or effort, not just bothered by the problem
    4. Problem discovery — whether enough interviews have been conducted to confirm the finding
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Willingness to solve describes whether a customer is actually motivated to spend money, time, or effort addressing a problem, as distinct from merely being bothered by it. A problem can be real, severe, and frequent, and a customer can still never act on it — which is exactly why the chapter treats willingness to solve as a separate check.

    19. A customer agrees a problem is real and even mildly bothersome, but says they've never paid for anything to address it because "rent and groceries come first." Which concept best explains this pattern?¶

    1. Problem urgency, because the customer does not want the problem solved quickly
    2. Essential bills priority — the tendency to direct limited money toward non-negotiable obligations before any discretionary spending
    3. Problem-solution fit, because no available solution matches the customer's root cause
    4. Perceived problem, because the customer has not actually experienced the difficulty firsthand
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Essential bills priority is the well-documented tendency for customers to direct limited money first toward non-negotiable obligations — rent, groceries, utilities, debt — before any discretionary spending on a problem that, however real, doesn't threaten their basic stability. It is one specific, common reason a confirmed real problem still fails to produce willingness to solve.

    20. After finishing her first round of interviews, Priya keeps talking to new customers each week, and her problem statement keeps getting sharper as a result. Which concept does this ongoing habit represent?¶

    1. Problem discovery — the ongoing research process of finding and refining problem statements through direct customer contact
    2. Problem prioritization — ranking multiple candidate problems against each other
    3. Problem validation — a one-time test of whether a perceived problem is genuinely felt
    4. Problem framing — deciding which words and angle to use in the problem statement
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Problem discovery is the ongoing research process of finding and refining problem statements through direct contact with customers — not a one-time task completed in an early session, but a habit that continues every time new field evidence complicates or sharpens what the founder thought they knew. Priya's continuing interviews are this habit in practice.
