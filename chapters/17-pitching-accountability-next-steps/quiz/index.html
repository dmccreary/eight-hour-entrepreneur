    Quiz: Pitching, Accountability & What Comes Next¶

    Test your understanding of the capstone pitch, accountability, and post-course support with these review questions.

    1. How does the chapter define the mini pitch?¶

    1. A written business plan submitted for instructor grading
    2. A private one-on-one meeting between a founder and a coach
    3. A pitch delivered only to potential investors outside the cohort
    4. A short, structured presentation of a founder's validated concept, canvas, and 30-day launch plan, delivered to the cohort as the course's capstone deliverable
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The mini pitch is a short, structured presentation of a founder's validated concept, canvas, and 30-day launch plan, delivered to the cohort as the course's capstone deliverable. It is not a written plan, a private coaching session, or an investor pitch — it is delivered out loud, to the cohort that has been together since Chapter 2.

    2. Why does the chapter say the two-minute pitch is useful specifically because it is time-boxed to 120 seconds?¶

    1. It gives founders more time than a typical investor pitch would allow
    2. It forces a founder to distill eleven canvas blocks and a full launch plan into the same message-simplicity discipline used in Chapter 12
    3. It eliminates the need for peer feedback afterward
    4. It replaces the need for a written 30-day launch plan
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The two-minute pitch is the mini pitch's specific time-boxed format, forcing a founder to distill eleven canvas blocks and a full launch plan into just 120 seconds — the same message-simplicity discipline from Chapter 12, now applied out loud. Peer feedback and the launch plan itself both still follow the pitch, not disappear because of it.

    3. After a founder's pitch, one cohort member asks a specific question about the evidence behind the price point, while another simply says "sounds great." Which response better applies the chapter's description of good peer feedback?¶

    1. The specific question about the evidence, because good peer feedback names something concrete rather than offering a generic reaction
    2. The "sounds great" comment, because it keeps the mood positive and non-judgmental
    3. Neither, because peer feedback should only be given in writing after the session
    4. Both equally, since either response satisfies psychological safety from Chapter 2
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Peer feedback consists of specific, evidence-based reactions from fellow founders responding to a pitch. Good peer feedback names something concrete — a question about the evidence, a suggestion for the ask — rather than a generic "sounds great," even though both responses can still follow the constructive, non-judgmental norms set in Chapter 2.

    4. Which of the following statements, spoken aloud to the cohort, best qualifies as a strong public commitment under the chapter's "specific enough to check" rule?¶

    1. "I'm going to keep growing the business as much as I can."
    2. "I hope to get a few more customers sometime soon."
    3. "I'll complete 8 driveway sessions by day 14."
    4. "I'll try to improve my pitch before the next cohort meets."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A public commitment is a specific, witnessed promise about what a founder will do in the next 30 days, drawn from the launch roadmap in Chapter 16. The chapter's own test is whether a peer could message the founder in two weeks and get a clear yes-or-no answer — "8 driveway sessions by day 14" passes that test, while the other options are too vague to check.

    5. What does the chapter say happens to a public commitment once Session 4 ends?¶

    1. It is submitted to MTN.org for formal review
    2. It becomes void unless a founder re-pitches it within 30 days
    3. It is replaced by the operational roadmap
    4. An accountability network — cohort peers, a coach, or both — continues checking in on the founder's progress against it
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. An accountability network is the group of people — cohort peers, a coach, or both — who continue checking in on a founder's progress against their public commitment after Session 4 concludes. The commitment doesn't expire or get replaced; the network is what keeps it alive after the live sessions end.

    6. The chapter distinguishes an accountability network from one specific relationship within it. What sets peer accountability apart from the broader accountability network?¶

    1. Peer accountability specifically describes cohort members checking in on each other, extending the peer learning community from Chapter 2
    2. Peer accountability refers only to check-ins from a paid coach, not from other founders
    3. Peer accountability ends the moment a coaching handoff occurs
    4. Peer accountability is a formal contract signed at Session 4
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Peer accountability specifically describes cohort members checking in on each other, extending the peer learning community from Chapter 2 past the course's own four weeks. The broader accountability network can also include a coach, but peer accountability is the piece that comes specifically from fellow founders.

    7. A founder's course facilitator stops providing structured weekly guidance, and the founder continues instead with lighter-touch peer and self-directed support. What does the chapter call this transition?¶

    1. Setback normalization
    2. A coaching handoff
    3. Launch readiness
    4. A milestone-driven plan
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A coaching handoff is the specific transition point where a course facilitator's structured guidance ends and a founder continues with lighter-touch peer or self-directed support instead. It is a single transition moment, distinct from the ongoing milestone-driven plan the founder follows afterward.

    8. According to the chapter, what is the community hub, and who provides it?¶

    1. A private messaging thread between a founder and their assigned coach only
    2. A one-time event held on the final day of the course
    3. The shared online space cohort members used throughout the course, provided by MTN.org
    4. A printed workbook distributed at the start of Session 1
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The community hub, provided by MTN.org, is the shared online space cohort members used throughout the course, which typically continues to be accessible afterward. It is a group space rather than a one-on-one coaching thread, a single event, or a printed handout.

    1. The Slack community is a separate, unrelated resource offered only to paying alumni
    2. The Slack community replaces the community hub after graduation
    3. The Slack community is the coaching handoff's formal replacement
    4. The Slack community is the specific platform hosting the ongoing community hub space
    Show Answer

It is not a separate resource or a replacement for coaching — it is the technical home of the same community hub already introduced in Chapter 2.

    14. One founder says they feel ready to launch because they're generally optimistic about the idea. Another says they feel ready because they have validated demand, a workable offer, and real evidence from their 30-day plan. Which founder's reasoning best reflects the chapter's definition of launch confidence?¶

    1. The second founder, because launch confidence is a grounded, evidence-based belief built on real validation rather than optimism alone
    2. The first founder, because optimism is what carries a venture through its first 30 days
    3. Both equally, since the chapter treats confidence and optimism as interchangeable
    4. Neither, because launch confidence can only be assessed by a coach
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Launch confidence is a founder's grounded, evidence-based belief that their venture is ready to continue past the course, based on real validation rather than optimism alone. The second founder's reasoning — validated demand, a workable offer, real evidence — is exactly what the chapter distinguishes from simple optimism.

    15. A graduating founder wants launch confidence to be backed by more than just their own belief. How would the chapter's concept of post-course support best be built into their plan?¶

    1. By relying solely on memory of what was taught in Sessions 1 through 4, without further outside input
    2. By deliberately keeping access open to coaching, the Slack community, and other resources available after graduation, so confidence doesn't have to stand alone
    3. By ending all contact with the cohort once the capstone presentation is delivered
    4. By waiting until a setback occurs before seeking out any further resources
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Post-course support is any continued access to coaching, community, or resources available to a founder after graduating from the live sessions, so launch confidence doesn't have to stand entirely alone. Deliberately keeping the Slack community and coaching accessible, rather than cutting contact or waiting for a setback, is what actually builds that support into the plan.
