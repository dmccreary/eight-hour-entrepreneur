    Quiz: Building the 30-Day Launch Plan¶

    Test your understanding of turning a completed canvas into a 30-day launch roadmap with these review questions.

    1. What is a thirty-day launch plan, as defined in this chapter?¶

    1. A multi-year business plan covering the venture's first three years of operation
    2. A single document listing only the venture's fixed and variable costs
    3. A concrete, week-by-week roadmap covering the 30 days immediately following the course, translating a completed canvas into scheduled action
    4. A pitch deck used to raise outside investment
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A thirty-day launch plan is a concrete, week-by-week roadmap covering the 30 days immediately following the course, translating a completed canvas into specific, scheduled actions rather than leaving validated ideas sitting on a page. It deliberately replaces the multi-year business plan and investor pitch deck this course avoids.

    2. How does the chapter distinguish a launch roadmap from a traditional multi-year business plan?¶

    1. A launch roadmap organizes 30 days of action into a sequence, replacing the multi-year plan this course deliberately avoids
    2. A launch roadmap covers the same time period as a multi-year business plan but with more financial detail
    3. A launch roadmap is only used after a venture has already achieved launch readiness
    4. A launch roadmap replaces the need for weekly milestones or targets
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The launch roadmap is the overall structure organizing 30 days of action into a sequence, replacing the multi-year theoretical business plan this course deliberately avoids, from Chapter 1, with something short enough to actually follow and revise. It still relies on weekly milestones and targets to structure that sequence.

    3. In the sample roadmap, Week 1 aims for "first 5 bookings confirmed." What is this an example of?¶

    1. A weekly target
    2. Launch readiness
    3. A money-saving pivot
    4. A weekly milestone
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A weekly milestone is a specific, meaningful checkpoint the plan aims to reach by the end of a given week — a concrete marker like "first 5 bookings confirmed," not a vague hope. The matching weekly target for that same week is the specific number, "5 sessions completed."

    7. How does the chapter define a weekly target?¶

    1. The specific number attached to a weekly milestone, set in advance so progress can be checked objectively rather than by feeling
    2. The overall structure organizing 30 days of action into a sequence
    3. A structured weekly reflection shared with cohort peers
    4. A small adjustment to the plan that reduces how much time a step requires
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A weekly target is the specific number attached to a weekly milestone, set in advance so progress can be checked objectively rather than by feeling — for example, "5 sessions completed" paired with the milestone "first 5 bookings confirmed." It is distinct from the launch roadmap's overall structure or a wins and lessons review.

    8. By day 30, Jordan has validated demand, a workable offer, and a functioning way to reach customers — but the venture is not yet perfect. Based on the chapter's definition, has Jordan reached launch readiness?¶

    1. No, because launch readiness requires a fully polished, error-free operation
    2. Yes, because launch readiness means enough validated evidence to continue operating with confidence, not perfection
    3. No, because launch readiness can only be confirmed by a course facilitator
    4. Yes, but only if every weekly target across all four weeks was hit exactly
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Launch readiness is a specific, evaluated state confirming the venture has enough validated demand, a workable offer, and a functioning way to reach customers to continue operating beyond the plan's initial 30 days — not perfection, just enough real evidence to keep going with confidence. Hitting every single target exactly is not required.

    9. Why does the chapter recommend stacking small, visible wins like a first booking or a first referral across the 30 days?¶

    1. To replace the need for weekly targets
    2. To satisfy a requirement of the launch roadmap template
    3. Because momentum building keeps progress motivating even before the venture is fully proven
    4. Because it eliminates the need for a wins and lessons review
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Momentum building is the deliberate practice of stacking small wins visibly across the 30 days — a first booking, a first referral — so progress stays motivating even before the venture is fully proven. It works alongside weekly targets and the wins and lessons review, not in place of either.

    10. Jordan's cohort meets each week to name what worked, what didn't, and what should change going forward. Which concept does this practice represent?¶

    1. Wins and lessons review
    2. Setback normalization
    3. Momentum building
    4. Launch readiness
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A wins and lessons review is a structured weekly reflection, ideally shared with cohort peers, that names what worked, what didn't, and what changes for next week — turning both wins and setbacks into forward motion rather than letting either one pass unexamined.

    11. Week 1 only produces 2 bookings instead of the target of 5. According to the chapter, how should this be treated?¶

    1. As a sign that the entire thirty-day launch plan should be abandoned
    2. As proof that the who-to-sell-to answer was wrong and must be replaced
    3. As evidence that a money-saving pivot is required immediately
    4. As an expected, normal part of the plan, treated like a lean test result rather than a sign something has gone wrong
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Setback normalization means treating a missed weekly target or a failed test as an expected, normal part of the plan rather than a sign something has gone wrong — directly extending the founder resilience and growth mindset built back in Chapter 2, and treating the miss like a lean test result rather than a verdict.

    12. Jordan is hitting every weekly target for bookings, but there simply aren't enough hours in the week to keep up with driving between driveway sessions. Which adjustment best fits the situation, and why?¶

    1. A money-saving pivot, because reducing supply costs will free up more selling time
    2. A time-saving pivot, such as batching sessions by neighborhood, because the scarce resource is founder time rather than money
    3. Abandoning the current who-to-sell-to answer and starting customer discovery over
    4. Increasing the weekly target so the shortfall looks smaller by comparison
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A time-saving pivot is a small adjustment to the plan that reduces how much founder time a step requires, such as batching driveway sessions by neighborhood to cut driving time. The chapter's own diagnostic is to ask which resource is actually scarce: here it is hours, not money, which points straight at a time-saving pivot rather than a money-saving one.

    13. Jordan's spending is outpacing the startup budget from Chapter 15, even though there's still enough time each week to complete sessions. How would Jordan best design a money-saving pivot to address this?¶

    1. Delay a planned equipment purchase and switch to a cheaper supplier for one recurring supply cost, without changing the sessions already scheduled
    2. Add more weekly milestones so progress is easier to track
    3. Simplify the booking process so fewer hours are spent per session
    4. Increase the price of every session immediately to cover the shortfall
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A money-saving pivot is a small adjustment that reduces what a step costs — the chapter's own examples are delaying a planned purchase or switching to a cheaper supplier for one ongoing cost. Since time is not the scarce resource here, a time-saving fix like simplifying the booking process would miss the actual constraint, which is spending outpacing the budget.
