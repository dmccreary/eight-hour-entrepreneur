    Quiz: Designing the Solution & Initial Offer¶

    Test your understanding of designing a testable initial offer with these review questions.

    1. How does the chapter define solution definition?¶

    1. A polished, investor-ready pitch describing every feature a venture will eventually build
    2. The practice of describing, in specific terms, what a venture will actually offer to address a validated problem
    3. A marketing slogan chosen to make the product sound appealing to a broad audience
    4. The final signed agreement between a founder and their first paying customer
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Solution definition is the practice of describing, in specific terms, what a venture will actually offer to address a validated problem — the third block on the canvas, and the first one where a founder starts building rather than researching. A strong solution definition traces back to the root cause identified in the Problem block, not to a polished pitch or slogan.

    2. Which statement best explains what makes something an initial offer rather than just a minimum viable offer?¶

    1. An initial offer includes every feature a founder eventually plans to build, while a minimum viable offer excludes them
    2. The two terms are identical and can be used interchangeably at any stage of testing
    3. An initial offer is the specific, concrete version of the minimum viable offer that a founder actually presents to real customers during the first round of field testing
    4. An initial offer is only used once a venture already has paying customers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. An initial offer is the specific, concrete version of the minimum viable offer that a founder actually presents to real customers during the very first round of field testing. Jordan's initial offer — a single dog wash and brush-out, at home, for $40, this week — narrows the broader minimum viable offer concept into one testable, bookable thing.

    3. What is a minimum viable offer, as the chapter defines it?¶

    1. The smallest, simplest version of a solution capable of genuinely solving the core problem, containing only what is essential to test whether the idea works
    2. A discounted version of the full product offered only to a venture's very first customers
    3. Any offer priced below what competitors currently charge for a similar solution
    4. The final, polished version of an offer after several rounds of solution iteration
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A minimum viable offer is the smallest, simplest version of a solution capable of genuinely solving the core problem for a target customer, containing only what is essential to test whether the underlying idea works — no polish, no edge cases, no nice-to-haves. It is not simply a discount or a final polished product.

    4. Which pairing correctly distinguishes a product offering from a service offering, using Jordan's example?¶

    1. Both a dog wash and a bag of pre-made treats are product offerings, because both involve a physical dog
    2. A product offering is always more expensive than a service offering
    3. Mobile dog grooming is a product offering because it is delivered at the customer's home
    4. A bagged batch of pre-made dog treats is a product offering — a tangible item produced once and sold repeatedly — while the grooming itself is a service offering
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A product offering is a tangible or standardized item the customer owns or consumes, produced once and sold repeatedly with little variation — like Jordan's bagged pre-made dog treats. Jordan's grooming itself is a service offering: a performed activity customized in the moment and consumed as it's delivered, not owned afterward.

    5. Why does the chapter classify Jordan's mobile grooming as a service offering rather than a product offering?¶

    1. Because it is delivered as a performed activity, customized in the moment to a specific customer's dog, and consumed as it's delivered rather than owned afterward
    2. Because it is produced once in a factory and then sold repeatedly with little variation
    3. Because customers can return it for a refund if they are unsatisfied with the result
    4. Because it does not require any direct interaction between Jordan and the customer
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A service offering is delivered as a performed activity, customized in the moment to a specific customer's situation, and consumed as it's delivered rather than owned afterward. Jordan's mobile grooming fits this exactly — each visit is performed live for a specific dog, unlike a standardized item produced once and sold repeatedly.

    6. In the CO.STARTERS Canvas's own phrasing, what question does the "what you offer" idea answer?¶

    1. How much the solution will cost the customer to purchase
    2. The specific plain-language description of the product or service a customer receives
    3. Which competitors already provide something similar in the market
    4. How the founder plans to advertise the solution to strangers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. "What you offer" is the CO.STARTERS Canvas's own phrasing for the Solution block's guiding question: the specific plain-language description of the product or service a customer receives. Jordan's draft business idea — mobile dog grooming, delivered at home, booked by text, paid at time of service — answers exactly this question.

    7. A founder designs an offer they personally find exciting to build, but it only loosely matches the validated problem and target customer from earlier chapters. What is missing?¶

    1. Offer clarity, because the pitch is too complicated for a stranger to understand
    2. Solution simplicity, because the offer includes too many features at once
    3. Value delivery, because the benefit is not being handed over on time
    4. Solution fit, because the offer needs to match the validated problem and customer rather than the founder's personal interest
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Solution fit measures how well the designed offer actually matches the validated problem and target customer from the previous chapters, rather than matching what a founder personally finds exciting to build. An offer built around a founder's own enthusiasm, without checking it against the validated problem and customer, has weak solution fit even if it is well executed.

    8. Jordan's offer includes text-message booking. Which statement correctly applies the feature-vs-benefit distinction to this detail?¶

    1. "Text-message booking" is the benefit, and "no phone tag, no waiting on hold" is the feature
    2. Both phrases describe the same feature, worded two different ways for marketing purposes
    3. "Text-message booking" is the feature — what the solution technically includes — and "no phone tag, no waiting on hold" is the benefit customers actually experience
    4. Neither phrase qualifies as a feature or a benefit; both describe the venture's core offering
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Feature vs. benefit distinguishes what a solution technically does or includes (a feature) from the positive outcome the customer actually experiences as a result (a benefit). "Text-message booking" is the feature; "no phone tag, no waiting on hold" is the benefit it produces for the customer — the two are related but not the same thing.

    9. What guides offer design, according to the chapter, when a founder decides which features, format, and terms to include?¶

    1. Whichever features are easiest or most interesting for the founder to build first
    2. Which benefits actually matter to the target customer, rather than which features are easiest to build
    3. The exact feature set already offered by the venture's closest direct competitor
    4. A fixed checklist of features required by every minimum viable offer regardless of the problem
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Offer design is the overall process of shaping which features, format, and terms make up a solution, guided by which benefits actually matter to the target customer — not by which features are easiest or most interesting for the founder to build. This keeps the offer anchored to customer value rather than founder convenience.

    10. After Jordan's first initial offer gets field-tested, Jordan adjusts the offer based on what customers said and tests the new version again. Which concept describes this repeated cycle?¶

    1. Solution refinement, because it only covers small, incremental wording changes
    2. Solution pivot, because Jordan abandoned the original approach entirely
    3. Solution assumption, because Jordan is still guessing what customers want
    4. Solution iteration, the repeated cycle of adjusting an offer based on field evidence and testing the adjusted version again
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Solution iteration is the repeated cycle of adjusting an offer based on field evidence, then testing the adjusted version again — the same evidence-based-decision-making habit applied specifically to the Solution block. It is a broader, ongoing cycle, distinct from solution refinement, which describes the smaller adjustments made within each iteration.

    11. How does the chapter define solution scope?¶

    1. The deliberate boundary a founder draws around what the offer will and will not include
    2. The total number of customers a venture hopes to serve within its first year
    3. The geographic area within which a founder is willing to deliver a service
    4. The list of every feature a founder might build once funding becomes available
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Solution scope is the deliberate boundary a founder draws around what the offer will and will not include. Drawing this boundary intentionally — rather than letting the offer grow to satisfy every possible version of the problem — is what makes a minimum viable offer possible in the first place.

    12. What is a draft business idea, as the chapter uses the term?¶

    1. A legally binding business plan submitted to investors before any customer interviews occur
    2. The final, most polished description of a venture's offering after solution testing is complete
    3. A rough, unpolished first pass at what the solution might be, written down specifically so it can be tested and changed
    4. A list of every competitor's offering, used as a template for the founder's own solution

A draft business idea is a rough, unpolished first pass at what the solution might be, written down specifically so it can be tested and changed rather than kept vague and unfalsifiable. Jordan's draft — mobile dog grooming, delivered at home, booked by text, paid at time of service — is an early, testable statement, not a finished business plan.

    13. Jordan tightens the wording of the initial offer and adds back one previously cut feature after customers say they'd like it. Which concept best describes this specific adjustment?¶

    1. Solution pivot, because Jordan is abandoning mobile grooming for a different approach
    2. Draft business idea, because Jordan is writing the very first version of the offer
    3. Solution assumption, because Jordan has not yet tested the adjustment with customers
    4. Solution refinement — the smaller, incremental adjustment within the iteration cycle, such as tightening scope or adding one validated feature back in
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Solution refinement describes the smaller, incremental adjustments made within the solution iteration cycle — tightening scope, clarifying the offer, or adding one validated feature back in. Jordan tightening wording and restoring a single customer-requested feature is exactly this scale of adjustment, distinct from the larger break of a solution pivot.

    14. Why does the chapter caution against a venture having three different core offerings at once?¶

    1. Because customers are legally required to choose only one offering per venture
    2. Because the resources meant to validate one thing get split three ways instead, usually leaving zero well-tested offerings
    3. Because product offerings and service offerings cannot legally coexist within the same venture
    4. Because a minimum viable offer can only ever contain a single feature
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter warns that an early-stage venture with three different core offerings usually ends up with zero well-tested offerings, because the limited resources meant to validate one thing get split three ways instead. Naming one clear core offering — the single primary product or service the venture is built around — keeps testing focused.

    15. Jordan promises customers "no phone tag, no waiting on hold," and then actually confirms every booking within minutes by text. Which concept does the actual, concrete confirmation represent?¶

    1. Offer clarity, because the pitch is stated plainly with no follow-up questions needed
    2. Feature vs. benefit, because it distinguishes what the offer includes from what it produces
    3. Value delivery — the mechanism by which a customer actually receives the promised benefit, not just what's promised
    4. Solution scope, because it defines the boundary of what the offer will include
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Value delivery is the mechanism by which a customer actually receives a promised benefit — not just what's promised, but how and when it's concretely handed over. Jordan's fast text confirmation is the concrete delivery of the "no phone tag" benefit, turning a promise made in the pitch into something the customer actually experiences.

    16. How does solution simplicity relate to solution scope, according to the chapter?¶

    1. Solution simplicity is the discipline of keeping scope as narrow as possible without breaking the offer's ability to actually solve the core problem
    2. Solution simplicity means removing every feature from an offer, including the ones essential to solving the core problem
    3. Solution simplicity only applies to product offerings, never to service offerings
    4. Solution simplicity is a synonym for offer clarity and does not involve scope at all
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Solution simplicity is the discipline of keeping solution scope as narrow as possible without breaking the offer's ability to actually solve the core problem. The two concepts work together: scope defines the boundary, and simplicity is the discipline of pushing that boundary as tight as it can go while still solving the problem.

    17. A founder is confident their offer will solve the customer's problem because they've imagined several scenarios in their head, but no real customer has said yes or no yet. What does the chapter call this belief?¶

    1. Draft business idea, because it is still an early, unpolished version of the offer
    2. Solution fit, because it has not yet been checked against the validated problem
    3. Solution assumption — an unverified belief that a specific offer will solve the customer's problem, true by hope rather than evidence
    4. Offer clarity, because the founder has not yet stated the offer plainly enough
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A solution assumption is an unverified belief that a specific offer will actually solve the customer's validated problem well enough that they'll choose it — true by hope, not yet by evidence. Imagining customer reactions does not resolve a solution assumption; only exposing it to real customer behavior, through solution testing, can do that.

    18. Which version of Jordan's pitch best demonstrates offer clarity?¶

    1. "A single dog wash and brush-out, at your home, for $40, this week."
    2. "Comprehensive at-home pet care solutions tailored to your unique needs."
    3. "Mobile grooming services available, pricing varies depending on several factors."
    4. "We handle everything your dog could possibly need, whenever you need it."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Offer clarity is the property of being stated so plainly and specifically that a customer immediately understands what they'd be getting and for how much, with no follow-up questions required. Jordan's "single dog wash and brush-out, at your home, for $40, this week" states exactly what, where, and how much — unlike the vaguer, jargon-filled alternatives.

    19. A founder has a solution assumption but has only tested it by imagining how customers might react. What does the chapter say still needs to happen before the assumption can be considered resolved?¶

    1. Solution refinement, to tighten the offer's scope before any customer sees it
    2. Offer design, to decide which features and terms belong in the solution
    3. Solution iteration, to adjust the offer repeatedly without customer contact
    4. Solution testing — exposing the assumption to real customer behavior, since imagining a reaction does not count as evidence
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Solution testing is the process of exposing a solution assumption to real customer behavior to find out whether it holds. Imagining how customers would react is explicitly called out as not counting — the chapter insists every solution assumption stays untested until a real person has said yes or no to the real initial offer.

    20. After months of testing, evidence shows Jordan's mobile-grooming approach cannot be refined into something that works, so Jordan abandons it for a completely different approach: selling grooming supplies online instead. Which concept describes this move?¶

    1. Solution refinement, because it is a small, incremental adjustment to the existing offer
    2. Solution pivot — abandoning the current solution approach entirely in favor of a substantially different one
    3. Solution iteration, because Jordan is testing an adjusted version of the same offer again
    4. Solution assumption, because Jordan has not yet gathered any evidence about the new approach
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A solution pivot is the larger move of abandoning the current solution approach entirely in favor of a substantially different one, made when evidence shows the current direction can't be refined into something that works, only replaced. Switching from mobile grooming to selling supplies online is a full change of approach, not an incremental refinement.
