    Quiz: The CO.STARTERS Canvas Framework¶

    Test your understanding of the CO.STARTERS Canvas framework with these review questions.

    1. What is the CO.STARTERS Canvas, as this chapter defines it?¶

    1. A multi-page business plan required before a venture can register with the state
    2. A single-page business-design tool made up of 11 linked sections, each holding the answer to one specific, plain-language question about a venture
    3. A financial spreadsheet that projects revenue and costs over five years
    4. A slide deck template used only during the Session 4 pitch
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The CO.STARTERS Canvas is a single-page business-design tool made up of 11 linked sections — Customer through Costs — each holding the answer to one specific, plain-language question. Unlike a multi-page business plan, it fits on one sheet by design, so a founder can see their whole business logic at a glance and a peer can review it in under two minutes.

    2. How does the chapter distinguish the CO.STARTERS Canvas from the Business Model Canvas?¶

    1. The Business Model Canvas has 11 blocks and the CO.STARTERS Canvas has 9
    2. The CO.STARTERS Canvas is used only by scaling companies, while the Business Model Canvas is used only by early-stage ventures
    3. The two canvases contain identical blocks, just arranged in a different order
    4. The Business Model Canvas assumes a business model roughly exists and asks you to describe it, while the CO.STARTERS Canvas assumes almost nothing exists yet and asks you to define it from a blank page
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The Business Model Canvas is built for established and scaling companies and assumes a business model roughly exists, asking you to describe it. The CO.STARTERS Canvas is a deliberate simplification for the early-stage moment, assuming almost nothing exists yet — which is why blocks like Advantage and Startup Needs, unique to founders who haven't started, have no direct equivalent on it.

    3. What is a canvas block?¶

    1. A single labeled section of the CO.STARTERS Canvas holding a short, specific answer to one guiding question, sized to fit a large index card
    2. A timed group exercise in which participants draft answers individually before comparing with a peer
    3. A full paragraph explaining one part of a venture's business plan
    4. A separate worksheet completed outside the live session
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A canvas block is a single labeled section of the CO.STARTERS Canvas holding a short, specific answer to one guiding question, deliberately scoped to fit a large index card rather than a full paragraph. If a block needs three paragraphs, the chapter says that usually means the founder hasn't reached a clear enough answer yet — not that the tool is too small.

    4. Why does the chapter treat the canvas sprint's strict time limit as a feature rather than a shortcut?¶

    1. Because a strict time limit guarantees the answer will be error-free
    2. Because it prevents any peer from reviewing the block before the next session
    3. Because the clock forces a founder to write an honest first-pass answer instead of endlessly polishing one block while the rest of the canvas sits empty
    4. Because it replaces the need for a Field Discovery Window that week
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A canvas sprint is a timed, focused group exercise — typically 5 to 10 minutes per block — where participants draft an answer individually before comparing with a peer. The time limit is a feature: it forces an honest first-pass answer instead of endlessly polishing one block while the rest of the canvas sits empty, exactly the idea-trap behavior Chapter 1 warned against.

    5. What does the Customer Block hold, according to Jordan's example, "busy dog owners within 5 miles without easy groomer access"?¶

    1. The specific pain that customer group feels, stated from their point of view
    2. A specific, falsifiable description of who the venture serves
    3. How the customer solves the problem today without the venture
    4. Why the customer wants the solution, in terms of their own outcome
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The Customer Block holds a specific, falsifiable description of who the venture serves — not "everyone with a dog," but a defined group. Jordan's answer, "busy dog owners within 5 miles without easy groomer access," is precise enough to guide every later decision on the canvas, which is why Chapter 4 devotes itself entirely to filling this block well.

    Concept Tested: Customer Block

    See: Glossary

    6. How should the Problem Block be stated, according to the chapter?¶

    1. As a one-line description the venture will use to pitch a stranger
    2. As the one-time resources the venture needs before it can begin
    3. As how the customer discovers and reaches the offer
    4. From the customer's point of view, rather than the founder's — distinct from a problem the founder only assumes exists
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The Problem Block holds the specific pain the customer group feels, stated from their point of view rather than the founder's — Chapter 5 covers how to tell a real problem from one a founder only assumes exists. Jordan's version, "30+ minute round trips, or skipping grooming altogether," names the customer's actual pain rather than Jordan's own preference.

    7. What does the Solution Block hold?¶

    1. How the customer solves the problem today without the venture's help
    2. Why the founder, specifically, is positioned to deliver the offer
    3. What the venture will actually offer to address the problem, kept deliberately small at first
    4. How the venture will charge for what it delivers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The Solution Block holds what the venture will actually offer to address the problem, and the chapter emphasizes keeping a first solution deliberately small. Jordan's answer, "grooming that comes to their driveway," is a minimal offer built to test the problem-solution fit before any larger investment, like a van, gets made.

    8. Jordan's Alternatives Block answer is "Drive to a booked-solid groomer, or do nothing." What does this illustrate about the Alternatives Block?¶

    1. It captures how the customer solves the problem today without the venture, including the option of doing nothing at all
    2. It captures the ongoing expenses the venture will carry once it's running
    3. It captures why the customer wants this solution specifically, in terms of their outcome
    4. It captures the one-time resources required before the venture can begin
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The Alternatives Block captures how the customer solves the problem today without the venture, including doing nothing at all. Jordan's answer, "drive to a booked-solid groomer, or do nothing," names the real competition the venture has to beat — not a competing dog-grooming van, but the customer's current, imperfect workaround.

    9. What does the Benefit Block hold?¶

    1. Why you, this particular founder, are positioned to deliver it better than anyone else
    2. The one-line way you'll describe the offer to a stranger
    3. How customers will actually discover and reach the offer
    4. Why the customer wants your solution specifically, in terms of their own outcome rather than your product's features
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The Benefit Block holds why the customer wants your solution specifically, framed in terms of their own outcome rather than your product's features. Jordan's answer, "no driving, no waiting for a booked calendar slot," describes what the customer gains, which the chapter pairs with the Advantage Block to build a complete value proposition in Chapter 8.

    10. Jordan's Advantage Block reads "Already trusted by 3 neighbors after driveway sessions." Why does the chapter say this block has no direct equivalent on the Business Model Canvas?¶

    1. Because the Business Model Canvas does not allow any block related to competitive positioning
    2. Because it answers why the founder, specifically, is positioned to solve this problem — a question that only matters to a founder who hasn't started yet
    3. Because Advantage only applies to B2B ventures, and the Business Model Canvas is B2C-only
    4. Because it duplicates the Customer Block and was removed from the Business Model Canvas for redundancy
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The Advantage Block holds why the founder specifically is positioned to deliver the solution better than anyone else. The chapter notes it has no direct equivalent on the Business Model Canvas because that question — why you, right now — only matters to a founder who hasn't started yet, unlike an established company mapping an existing operation.

    2. The ongoing expenses the venture will carry once it's running
    3. How customers will actually discover and reach the offer
    4. How the venture will charge for what it delivers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The Message Block holds the one-line way you'll describe the offer to a stranger, covered in depth in Chapter 12. Jordan's answer, "Dog grooming that comes to your driveway," compresses the Customer, Problem, and Solution blocks into a single sentence a stranger can immediately understand.

    14. How does the chapter distinguish the Startup Needs Block from its neighbor, the Costs Block?¶

    1. Startup Needs holds ongoing monthly expenses, while Costs holds one-time equipment purchases
    2. Startup Needs applies only to B2B ventures, while Costs applies only to B2C ventures
    3. Startup Needs holds the one-time resources required before the venture can begin, while Costs holds the ongoing expenses it carries once running
    4. Startup Needs is filled in during Session 1, while Costs is filled in only during Session 4
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The Startup Needs Block holds the one-time resources required before the venture can begin at all — equipment, a license, a first batch of supplies — while its neighbor, the Costs Block, holds the ongoing expenses the venture carries once it's running, such as materials, gas, or subscriptions. Chapter 15 covers both blocks together.

    15. Jordan lists "Supplies, gas, insurance" on the canvas. Which block does this answer belong in?¶

    1. Startup Needs Block
    2. Costs Block
    3. Revenue Block
    4. Advantage Block
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Jordan's ongoing expenses — supplies, gas, insurance — belong in the Costs Block, distinct from the Startup Needs Block, which holds one-time items like a grooming kit and business cards. Separating one-time from ongoing expenses lets a founder see both what it takes to begin and what it takes to keep operating.

    16. A canvas names "busy working parents" as the Customer Block but lists "flyers posted at the senior center" as the Distribution Block. What does canvas synthesis reveal about this canvas?¶

    1. The blocks don't add up as one coherent argument — the message will never reach the customer it claims to be for
    2. Nothing is wrong, since each block is individually a reasonable, complete answer
    3. The canvas simply needs a longer Costs Block to resolve the mismatch
    4. The mismatch only matters if the venture is B2B rather than B2C
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Canvas synthesis is reading all 11 completed blocks together as one coherent argument, checking that each block's answer supports its neighbors rather than contradicting them. A Customer block naming "busy working parents" paired with a Distribution block of "flyers at the senior center" is exactly the mismatch synthesis catches — the message would never reach the stated customer.
