    Quiz: Entrepreneurship, Lean Thinking & the Idea Trap¶

    Test your understanding of lean startup thinking and the idea trap with these review questions.

    1. How does this chapter define entrepreneurship?¶

    1. The process of securing venture capital funding to scale a business quickly
    2. The management of an established company's day-to-day operations
    3. The practice of turning an unproven idea into an ongoing activity that creates value real enough that a customer is willing to pay for it
    4. The act of writing a formal business plan before approaching any customer
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter defines entrepreneurship broadly: turning an unproven idea into an ongoing activity that creates value real enough that a customer will pay for it. This applies to a consultant leaving a salaried job, a nonprofit founder, or Jordan thinking through a mobile grooming van — the moment any of them seriously start asking whether the idea could work, they are practicing entrepreneurship, long before money changes hands or a formal company exists.

    2. Why does the chapter define an early-stage venture as a business whose core assumptions are still unproven?¶

    1. Because that definition tells you what kind of work is appropriate right now — testing whether the idea works, not scaling an already-proven model
    2. Because early-stage ventures are legally required to operate without paying customers for their first year
    3. Because investors will not fund a venture until it has thousands of loyal customers
    4. Because an early-stage venture must first build a complete, polished product before approaching any customer
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. An early-stage venture is a business where its core assumptions are still unproven — few or no paying customers and no repeatable way to acquire more. That definition matters because it tells a founder what kind of work is appropriate right now: testing whether the idea works, like Jordan's grooming-van thought, rather than scaling and optimizing an already-proven business model.

    3. What is an "unrefined concept" as the chapter uses the term, illustrated by Jordan's initial dog-grooming-van thought?¶

    1. A business idea that has already been tested with paying customers but failed
    2. A fully written business plan waiting only for funding
    3. A polished pitch deck that has not yet been shown to investors
    4. A business idea that exists only as a general direction, with none of its key details worked out
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. An unrefined concept, like Jordan's "someone should drive a grooming van to people's houses" thought, exists only as a general direction. Key details — who the customer is, exactly what's offered, how it charges — haven't been worked out yet. That openness is exciting precisely because none of those questions have been answered wrong yet either.

    4. How does the chapter distinguish a "business idea" from an "unrefined concept"?¶

    1. A business idea is legally registered, while an unrefined concept is not
    2. A business idea is the more general term for any proposed venture for creating and delivering value in exchange for payment, used once the unrefined concept starts getting shaped into something concrete
    3. A business idea always includes a completed financial projection, while an unrefined concept never does
    4. A business idea has already been validated by real customers, while an unrefined concept has not
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A business idea is the more general term the chapter uses once an unrefined concept starts getting shaped into something concrete — any proposed venture for creating and delivering value to a customer in exchange for payment. It's broader than a specific tested claim, but more concrete than a vague direction like Jordan's original hunch.

    5. The Idea Trap Cycle diagram contrasts a closed loop with a forward path. Which activity keeps a founder inside the idea trap's closed loop rather than moving them toward validated learning?¶

    1. Knocking on a stranger's door to ask whether they would pay for the offer
    2. Running a small, real-world test like a driveway grooming session for a fee
    3. Calling five neighbors to ask about their current spending on the problem
    4. Refining the pitch, logo, or financial projections instead of testing the idea against a real customer
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. In the Idea Trap Cycle, refining the pitch, logo, or spreadsheets forms a closed loop that feeds back into the unrefined idea without ever testing it. It feels productive but changes nothing about whether a customer actually wants the offer. Knocking on doors, running driveway sessions, and calling neighbors all belong to the forward path toward validated learning instead.

    6. In the Clarity → Validation → Action → Launch cycle, what does "Clarity" mean?¶

    1. Having a fully validated business model with paying customers
    2. Producing a polished slide deck that explains the venture to investors
    3. Being able to state, in one or two plain sentences, who you believe your customer is and what problem you believe they have
    4. Committing to a first real version of the business in front of actual customers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Clarity means being able to state, in one or two plain sentences, who you believe your customer is and what problem you believe they have — an honest, falsifiable guess, not a polished mission statement. Jordan's version was: "I think busy dog owners without easy grooming access would pay for grooming that comes to them."

    7. How does Jordan's phone calls to five neighbors illustrate "Validation" in the four-phase cycle?¶

    1. They test a guess about the customer and problem against reality by talking to real people, instead of assuming the guess is correct because it feels right
    2. They represent the smallest real-world action that produces evidence, replacing a bigger safer plan
    3. They commit Jordan to a first real offer in front of actual customers
    4. They state, in one sentence, who Jordan believes the customer is
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Validation means testing a guess against reality by talking to and eventually transacting with real people, rather than assuming it's correct because it feels right. Jordan's five neighbor calls did exactly that — checking what their current grooming routine actually costs them in time and money, instead of assuming busy dog owners would obviously want a driveway service.

    8. What does the chapter mean by "Action" in the four-phase cycle?¶

    1. Stating a falsifiable guess about the customer and the problem they have
    2. Doing the smallest real-world thing that produces evidence, instead of the safer, bigger thing of building a complete product first
    3. Testing a guess against reality through customer conversations
    4. Committing to a first real version of the business in front of actual customers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Action means doing the smallest real-world thing that produces evidence — sending one message, asking one stranger to pay — instead of the safer, bigger move of building a complete product first. Jordan's action was borrowing a friend's car and grooming one neighbor's dog for $40, rather than buying a van before knowing anyone would pay.

    9. Why does the chapter argue that an incomplete offer "launched" into the real world teaches a founder more than a perfect offer that never leaves the notebook?¶

    1. Because a launched offer no longer requires any further validation
    2. Because customers only trust offers that are visibly unfinished
    3. Because putting an incomplete offer in front of actual customers generates real evidence in a week that a hidden, polished plan cannot generate in a year
    4. Because launching removes the need to ever revisit Clarity or Validation again
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Launch means committing to a first real version of the business and putting it in front of actual customers, even incomplete, because an incomplete offer teaches more in a week of real contact than a perfect, hidden offer teaches in a year. Jordan's launch was posting the driveway offer to the whole neighborhood app after three successful sessions.

    10. How does the chapter describe the relationship between the Clarity → Validation → Action → Launch cycle and lean startup philosophy?¶

    1. Lean startup philosophy replaces the four-phase cycle once a venture has paying customers
    2. The four-phase cycle applies only to technology startups, while lean startup philosophy applies to all businesses
    3. Lean startup philosophy is a later stage that begins only after Launch is complete
    4. The four-phase cycle is this course's specific expression of lean startup philosophy — building and testing a venture through small, fast, low-cost experiments rather than one large upfront investment
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Lean startup philosophy is the practice of building and testing a venture through small, fast, low-cost experiments rather than one large upfront investment based on untested assumptions. The chapter's Clarity → Validation → Action → Launch cycle is this course's specific expression of that broader idea — minimizing the waste of building something elaborate before knowing it's wanted.

    11. A founder is deciding whether to raise their price. Which choice is the clearest example of evidence-based decision making, as this chapter defines it?¶

    1. Raising the price because a friend says it sounds too cheap for the amount of work involved
    2. Reviewing that 3 of 5 neighbors said yes to the current price before deciding whether to test a higher one
    3. Keeping the price the same because changing it feels risky
    4. Setting the price based on what seems logical for a service like this
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Evidence-based decision making means using data gathered from real customer behavior rather than internal debate, personal conviction, or what merely sounds reasonable. Checking that 3 of 5 neighbors actually said yes at the current price, before deciding whether to test a higher one, uses real behavioral data — instinct can generate the hypothesis, but evidence answers it.

    12. According to the chapter, what is an assumption?¶

    1. Any belief about your customer, problem, or business that you have not yet tested against real-world evidence
    2. A belief that has already been confirmed through a customer's paid transaction
    3. A written business plan that has not yet been reviewed by a peer
    4. A statement that has been disproven by real-world testing
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. An assumption is any belief about your customer, problem, or business that hasn't yet been tested against real-world evidence. Assumptions aren't a sign of poor planning — every business idea starts out built entirely of them, because that's what "unproven" means. "Busy dog owners will pay $40 for driveway grooming" was an assumption before Jordan tested it.

    13. Jordan calls five neighbors and none of them agree to pay $40 for a driveway grooming session. According to the chapter's definition, is this result validated learning?¶

    1. No — validated learning only occurs when a test confirms the original guess was correct
    2. No — this is still just an assumption, since only five people were asked
    3. Yes, but only because Jordan already owns a van and can absorb the loss
    4. Yes — validated learning does not require the test to succeed; the disproven result still tells Jordan something needs to change before buying a van
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Validated learning is what an assumption becomes once it's been tested against reality and the result — confirmed or disproven — is known with reasonable confidence. The word "learning" does real work here: the test doesn't need to succeed. A disproven price or offer is still validated learning, arriving before a van gets purchased rather than after.
