## What Is a Distribution Channel

A **distribution channel** is any specific path or method through which a customer discovers, learns about, and ultimately accesses a venture's offer — the fifth canvas block, and the one this chapter is devoted to. Choosing which channels to use is part of a venture's broader **go-to-market approach**: the overall strategy for reaching and acquiring customers, combining one or more distribution channels with the messaging from Chapter 12 into a coherent plan.

Every channel choice ultimately shapes a specific **customer acquisition path**: the actual sequence of steps a real customer follows from first encountering the venture to becoming a paying customer — seeing a neighborhood app post, texting a question, booking a time slot, paying at the door, for Jordan's grooming service.

## Direct, Online, and In-Person Channels

Distribution channels come in several broad shapes. A **direct sales channel** involves the founder personally reaching out to and closing individual customers, one at a time, without an intermediary. **Online distribution** reaches customers through internet-based platforms — websites, apps, search, or online marketplaces — where a customer can discover and often complete a purchase without any in-person contact. **In-person distribution** reaches customers through physical presence — a booth at a local event, a flyer on a community board, a conversation at the dog park — relying on physical proximity rather than a screen.

## Five Specific Channel Types

Within those broad shapes, five more specific channel types recur across almost every early-stage venture.

| Channel | How It Works | Jordan's or Priya's Example |
|---------|---------------|-------------------------------|
| Word of Mouth | Existing customers tell others without being asked | 3 driveway customers mentioning Jordan to neighbors |
| Referral Channel | Existing customers are deliberately incentivized to refer others | "$10 off your next session for every friend you refer" |
| Partnership Channel | Another business with the same target customer promotes you | A local pet store agreeing to display Jordan's flyer |
| Marketplace Channel | A third-party platform lists your offer alongside competitors | Listing grooming availability on a local services app |
| Social Media Channel | Customers discover you through social platforms | Priya posting meal photos in a local parents' group |

**Word of mouth** is the specific channel where existing, satisfied customers tell others about a venture without being deliberately prompted or incentivized to do so. A **referral channel** is a deliberate system that encourages and rewards existing customers for bringing in new ones — the same underlying behavior as word of mouth, but designed and incentivized rather than left to chance. A **partnership channel** reaches customers through an agreement with another business that already has access to the same target customer. A **marketplace channel** reaches customers through a third-party platform that lists many similar offers together, trading some independence for built-in customer traffic. A **social media channel** reaches customers through content posted on social platforms, whether through organic posts, groups, or paid promotion.

## Choosing the Right Channel: Cost, Reach, and Fit

With several channel types on the table, a founder has to choose. **Channel selection** is the deliberate process of choosing which distribution channels to actually pursue, rather than trying every option available at once. Three properties guide that choice. **Channel cost** is what a channel actually requires to use — money, time, or both. **Channel reach** is how many of your target customers a channel can realistically expose your offer to. **Channel fit** is how well a specific channel matches where your actual target customer already spends their attention — the best channel isn't the cheapest or the widest-reaching one in the abstract, it's the one your specific customer from Chapter 4 is already paying attention to.

## Testing Channels and Going Multi-Channel

Like every other canvas block, a channel choice is an assumption until proven. **Channel testing** is running a small, deliberate trial of a specific channel — posting in one Facebook group, asking for one referral — and measuring the actual response before committing further budget or time to it. Once one or two channels show real results, a venture may adopt a **multi-channel strategy**: deliberately using more than one distribution channel at once, chosen because they reach different, complementary parts of the target customer base rather than randomly.

!!! mascot-warning "Don't Spread Across Five Channels Before Testing One"
    ![Scout warning](../../img/mascot/warning.png){ class="mascot-admonition-img" }
    A common trap here is launching a social media page, a partnership pitch, a referral program, and a marketplace listing all in the same week. The fix: channel-test one at a time. An early-stage venture rarely has the time or budget to run five experiments simultaneously — and mixed results across five channels are impossible to interpret cleanly.

## Key Takeaways

Before Chapter 14 turns attention to how the venture actually charges, hold onto how a message finds its customer:

- A **distribution channel** is one path within a venture's overall **go-to-market approach**, shaping the real **customer acquisition path** a customer follows.
- **Direct sales**, **online**, and **in-person distribution** are the broad shapes; **word of mouth**, **referral**, **partnership**, **marketplace**, and **social media channels** are specific types within them.
- **Channel selection** weighs **channel cost**, **channel reach**, and **channel fit** together — never cost or reach alone.
- **Channel testing** proves a channel works before committing further, and a genuine **multi-channel strategy** is built from tested channels, not assumed ones.
