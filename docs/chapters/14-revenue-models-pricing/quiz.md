#### 20. A founder wants to design a simple monthly framework that tells them, at a glance, exactly how many sales they need before the venture stops losing money each month, using price, cost per sale, and fixed monthly costs. How should the founder construct this framework, based on this chapter's break-even thinking?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Track only total revenue each month and ignore fixed costs entirely
2. Set the price equal to the cost per sale so every sale is automatically profitable
3. Build the calculation as Fixed Monthly Costs divided by (Price per Sale minus Cost per Sale), recalculating whenever any of the three inputs changes
4. Estimate break-even from an optimistic sales conversion rate rather than the three cost and price inputs
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Break-even thinking is the discipline of calculating the point at which total revenue exactly covers total costs, built from price, cost per sale, and fixed monthly costs working together. Structuring it as Fixed Monthly Costs divided by (Price per Sale minus Cost per Sale) — recalculated whenever an input changes — is exactly the framework this chapter describes, and it also guards against the case where price never exceeds cost.

    **Concept Tested:** Break-Even Thinking

    **See:** [Glossary](../../glossary.md)
