# References: Alternatives & the Competitive Landscape

1. [Competitor Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competitor_analysis) - Wikipedia - Explains the systematic evaluation of existing and potential competitors' strategies, strengths, and weaknesses, the foundation for this chapter's competitive research and competitive analysis concepts.
2. [Substitute Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_good) - Wikipedia - Covers the economic concept of interchangeable products that satisfy the same underlying need, directly relevant to this chapter's substitute solution category of alternative.
3. [Positioning (marketing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positioning_(marketing)) - Wikipedia - Describes how a company shapes where its offer sits in a customer's mind relative to rivals, the concept this chapter's competitive positioning builds toward.
4. Competing Against Luck - Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, David S. Duncan - HarperBusiness - Credited for Jobs to Be Done theory and the insight that a venture's real competition is often "non-consumption," which explains why this chapter treats the do-nothing alternative as the toughest competitor.
5. Competitive Strategy: Techniques for Analyzing Industries and Competitors - Michael E. Porter - Free Press - Credited for the Five Forces framework and the systematic method of classifying rivals, substitutes, and market structure that underlies this chapter's direct/indirect/substitute competitor spectrum.
6. [Market Research and Competitive Analysis](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/market-research-competitive-analysis) - U.S. Small Business Administration - Explains how a small business identifies competitors and evaluates their strengths and weaknesses to find a competitive edge, mirroring this chapter's alternative assessment process.
7. [Competitive Analysis](https://www.entrepreneur.com/encyclopedia/competitive-analysis) - Entrepreneur Small Business Encyclopedia - Defines competitive analysis as identifying competitors and evaluating their strategies to find what makes an offer unique, a plain-language companion to this chapter's competitive landscape mapping.
8. [The Five Competitive Forces That Shape Strategy](https://hbr.org/2008/01/the-five-competitive-forces-that-shape-strategy) - Harvard Business Review - Michael Porter's own account of how rivals, substitutes, and new entrants shape industry structure, giving founders the original framework behind this chapter's competitor categories.
9. [The Past and Future of Competitive Advantage](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-past-and-future-of-competitive-advantage/) - MIT Sloan Management Review - Clayton Christensen's argument that a competitive edge only holds under specific, changing conditions, reinforcing this chapter's point that differentiation must be grounded in a real, confirmed market gap.
10. [Business Model Design for 21st Century Companies](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/business-model-design-for-21st-century-companies) - Strategyzer - Shows how Taobao, Michelin, and Airbnb each found an unmet gap in a crowded market and differentiated around it, illustrating this chapter's market gap and differentiation concepts with real companies.
