# References: Building the 30-Day Launch Plan

1. [Business plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_plan) - Wikipedia - Describes the traditional formal business plan's goals, methods, and timeframes, giving useful contrast for this chapter's short, action-oriented 30-day launch plan that deliberately replaces a multi-year business plan.

2. [Milestone (project management)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestone_(project_management)) - Wikipedia - Explains how milestones mark specific checkpoints along a project timeline for reviews and budget verification, the same function this chapter's weekly milestones and weekly targets serve across the 30-day plan.

3. [Lean startup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_startup) - Wikipedia - Covers the build-measure-learn cycle and defines a pivot as a structured, evidence-based course correction rather than a failure, directly underlying this chapter's time-saving and money-saving pivots and setback normalization.

4. Scaling Up (Rockefeller Habits 2.0) - Verne Harnish - Gazelles, Inc. - Originated a system of quarterly and weekly "Rocks" and milestone-driven execution rhythms that turn a strategic plan into checkable weekly targets, the direct ancestor of this chapter's weekly-milestone structure for the 30-day plan.

5. The Lean Startup - Eric Ries - Crown Business - Defined "pivot" as a structured, hypothesis-driven course correction validated by evidence rather than a sign of failure, the origin of this chapter's time-saving pivot, money-saving pivot, and setback normalization concepts.

6. [Why the Lean Start-Up Changes Everything](https://hbr.org/2013/05/why-the-lean-start-up-changes-everything) - Harvard Business Review - Steve Blank's argument that rapid experimentation and iteration should replace the traditional plan-fund-build-launch sequence, reinforcing this chapter's case for a short, revisable roadmap over a theoretical business plan.

7. [An MVP Is Not a Cheaper Product, It's About Smart Learning](https://steveblank.com/2013/07/22/an-mvp-is-not-a-cheaper-product-its-about-smart-learning/) - Steve Blank - Argues that a minimum viable product should test the core customer-demand hypothesis as cheaply as possible, informing this chapter's "what to sell" question as a concrete, testable 30-day offer.

8. [Write your business plan](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/write-your-business-plan) - U.S. Small Business Administration - Compares traditional and lean startup business plan formats, useful context for understanding how this chapter's 30-day launch roadmap trades comprehensive planning for scheduled, near-term action.

9. [Your Business Strategy Suddenly Stopped Working. Here's How to Pivot Without Panicking.](https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/how-to-pivot-when-your-business-strategy-starts-failing-you/500544) - Entrepreneur.com - Offers a framework for diagnosing a missed target and making small, controlled adjustments rather than a full overhaul, closely matching this chapter's time-saving and money-saving pivot distinction.

10. [How to Use Milestones to Create a Roadmap for Your Business](https://www.liveplan.com/blog/planning/milestones-as-business-roadmap) - LivePlan - Explains how to set milestones across plan review, assumption validation, and implementation categories and revisit them frequently, a practical parallel to this chapter's weekly milestone and weekly target structure.
