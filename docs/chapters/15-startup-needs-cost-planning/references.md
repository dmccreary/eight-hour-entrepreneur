# References: Startup Needs & Cost Planning

1. [Fixed cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_cost) - Wikipedia - Defines costs that stay constant regardless of output and contrasts them with variable costs, providing the underlying economic definitions behind this chapter's fixed-versus-variable cost distinction.

2. [Cash flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_flow) - Wikipedia - Explains how money moves into and out of a business over time, distinct from profit, forming the conceptual basis for this chapter's cash flow awareness and cost tracking practices.

3. [Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget) - Wikipedia - Covers how organizations and individuals plan spending against expected resources, including personal and corporate budgeting types, relevant to this chapter's startup budget and minimal viable budget concepts.

4. Profit First - Mike Michalowicz - Portfolio/Penguin - Originated the "Profit First" cash-allocation method, in which revenue is divided into separate bank accounts by percentage before any spending happens, operationalizing this chapter's cash flow awareness and cost tracking as a concrete daily habit.

5. The $100 Startup - Chris Guillebeau - Crown Business - Documented real minimal-capital launch budgets across dozens of one-person businesses, giving empirical weight to this chapter's needs-vs-wants assessment and its case for a lean, no-padding minimal viable budget.

6. [Calculate your startup costs](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/calculate-your-startup-costs) - U.S. Small Business Administration - Walks through identifying one-time and monthly business expenses and organizing them into a cost worksheet, a direct real-world counterpart to this chapter's cost structure and cost estimation steps.

7. [How to Estimate Starting Costs](https://www.sba.gov/blog/how-estimate-starting-costs) - U.S. Small Business Administration - Explains how to research realistic dollar figures for pre-launch expenses, required assets, and operating cash reserves, supporting this chapter's emphasis on cost estimation grounded in real numbers rather than guesses.

8. [Bootstrapping Your Startup: A Business Guide for Entrepreneurs](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/business-planning/bootstrapping-your-startup-a-business-guide-for-entrepreneurs) - J.P. Morgan - Defines bootstrapping as funding a company with personal finances or operating revenue instead of outside investment, and offers practical tips for staying lean, directly supporting this chapter's bootstrapped funding and startup capital concepts.

9. [Cash Flow Management](https://www.score.org/cash-flow-management/) - SCORE - Explains why more small businesses fail from poor cash flow management than from lack of profit, with guidance on short-term forecasting, reinforcing this chapter's warning that being profitable on paper is not the same as having cash in the bank.

10. [Startup Financing Trends by Race: How Access to Capital Impacts Profitability](https://www.kauffman.org/entrepreneurship/reports/startup-financing-trends-by-race-how-access-to-capital-impacts-profitability/) - Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation - Research finding that personal and family savings fund nearly two-thirds of new businesses, far more than bank loans or investors, grounding this chapter's bootstrapped-funding default in real financing data.
