# References: Designing the Solution & Initial Offer

1. [Minimum Viable Product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_viable_product) - Wikipedia - Explains the concept of a product with just enough features to satisfy early customers and provide feedback, directly underlying this chapter's minimum viable offer and initial offer.
2. [Lean Startup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_startup) - Wikipedia - Describes the build-measure-learn methodology for developing businesses and products through validated learning, the framework this chapter's solution testing and iteration cycle is built on.
3. [New Product Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_product_development) - Wikipedia - Covers the full process of bringing a new offering to market through idea generation, design, and testing, providing context for how a draft business idea becomes a core offering.
4. The Lean Startup - Eric Ries - Crown Business - Credited for the Build-Measure-Learn loop and the minimum viable product technique of shipping the smallest testable version of an idea, the direct ancestor of this chapter's minimum viable offer and solution-testing cycle.
5. Running Lean (3rd Edition) - Ash Maurya - O'Reilly Media - Credited for the Lean Canvas and the discipline of stripping a plan down to its riskiest assumptions before building, clarifying why this chapter insists on a narrow solution scope rather than a fully featured offer.
6. [Finding Product-Market Fit in the Tech Industry](https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/how-to-find-product-market-fit) - Harvard Business School Online - Defines an MVP as the smallest feature set that delivers the fastest learning, reinforcing this chapter's emphasis on cutting an offer down before testing it with real customers.
7. [Why the Lean Start-Up Changes Everything](https://hbr.org/2013/05/why-the-lean-start-up-changes-everything) - Harvard Business Review - Steve Blank's overview of how validated, evidence-based iteration replaces upfront business planning, paralleling this chapter's solution assumption, testing, and refinement cycle.
8. [Write Your Business Plan](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/write-your-business-plan) - U.S. Small Business Administration - Walks through describing a product or service line in plain, specific terms, matching this chapter's offer clarity requirement for a testable initial offer.
9. [Perfection By Subtraction – The Minimum Feature Set](https://steveblank.com/2010/03/04/perfection-by-subtraction-the-minimum-feature-set/) - Steve Blank - Argues that a minimum feature set exists to reduce wasted effort and get a testable offer in front of early customers quickly, directly supporting this chapter's solution simplicity and scope discipline.
10. [How to Build an MVP](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Io-how-to-build-an-mvp) - Y Combinator Startup Library - Walks founders through determining an MVP's feature set and presenting it to early customers, a practical companion to this chapter's process of narrowing a draft idea into an initial offer.
