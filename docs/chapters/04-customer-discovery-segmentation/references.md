# References: Customer Discovery & Segmentation

1. [Market segmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_segmentation) - Wikipedia - Comprehensive coverage of segmentation bases and targeting approaches, the marketing-theory foundation this chapter draws on when narrowing a broad market into a customer segment and niche market.

2. [Early adopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_adopter) - Wikipedia - Explains the early-adopter concept and its roots in Everett Rogers's technology adoption lifecycle, directly supporting this chapter's advice to design a first offer for early adopters rather than the cautious mainstream customer.

3. [Buyer persona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyer_persona) - Wikipedia - Covers the history and use of buyer personas as semi-fictional customer representations, tracing the concept this chapter uses to distinguish a customer persona from an ideal customer profile and a customer avatar.

4. Marketing Management (15th Edition) - Philip Kotler and Kevin Lane Keller - Pearson - Kotler pioneered the Segmentation-Targeting-Positioning (STP) framework in this defining marketing textbook, the exact narrowing sequence this chapter walks through from broad market to niche market to target customer.

5. Crossing the Chasm - Geoffrey A. Moore - HarperBusiness - Moore pioneered the technology adoption lifecycle's market application, explaining why early adopters and mainstream customers behave so differently, the direct source of this chapter's advice to sell to early adopters first.

6. [The Guide to Personas](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/persona/) - Nielsen Norman Group - Explains how research-grounded personas turn abstract user statistics into a memorable, decision-ready individual, supporting this chapter's walkthrough of building a customer persona from real interview data.

7. [The Importance of Target Audience in Marketing Strategy](https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/target-audience-in-marketing) - Harvard Business School Online - Explains how demographic data, behavior, and motivation combine to define a target audience, reinforcing this chapter's four-part breakdown of customer needs, behavior, motivation, and journey.

8. [Market Research](https://www.score.org/market-research/) - SCORE - Frames market research as answering whether a real, paying market exists, and stresses direct customer conversations over data alone, supporting this chapter's emphasis on testing a customer definition rather than assuming it.

9. [Market Segmentation and Targeting](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/management/market-segmentation-and-targeting/) - Corporate Finance Institute - Walks through the Segmentation-Targeting-Positioning process and five segmentation methods, giving concrete detail behind this chapter's narrowing sequence from broad market to serviceable market.

10. [Market Research and Competitive Analysis](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/market-research-competitive-analysis) - U.S. Small Business Administration - Explains primary versus secondary research methods for defining a target market's size, demographics, and trends, supporting this chapter's customer-identification step of confirming a target customer with real evidence.
