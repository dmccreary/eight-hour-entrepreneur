# References: Entrepreneurship, Lean Thinking & the Idea Trap

1. [Entrepreneurship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrepreneurship) - Wikipedia - Comprehensive overview of entrepreneurship's definition, history, and psychological dimensions, supporting this chapter's broad definition of entrepreneurship as turning an unproven idea into customer value, beyond the venture-capital stereotype.

2. [Lean startup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_startup) - Wikipedia - Detailed explanation of the lean startup methodology, including the build-measure-learn loop, minimum viable product, and validated learning — the exact vocabulary this chapter introduces as the course's operating philosophy.

3. [Startup company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company) - Wikipedia - Covers what defines a startup versus an established business, including funding stages and failure patterns, reinforcing this chapter's definition of an early-stage venture as one with unproven core assumptions.

4. The Lean Startup - Eric Ries - Crown Business - Ries pioneered the Build-Measure-Learn loop and the concept of validated learning through a minimum viable product, giving this chapter's Clarity-Validation-Action-Launch cycle its direct methodological ancestor.

5. The Startup Owner's Manual - Steve Blank and Bob Dorf - K&S Ranch Publishing - Blank originated the Customer Development process and the "get out of the building" discipline, the exact antidote this chapter prescribes for escaping the idea trap through real customer contact.

6. [Why the Lean Start-Up Changes Everything](https://hbr.org/2013/05/why-the-lean-start-up-changes-everything) - Harvard Business Review - Steve Blank explains how lean startup practices replace an untested business plan with rapid, low-cost experiments, directly reinforcing this chapter's contrast between planning and evidence-based validation.

7. [Building a Minimum Viable Product? You're Probably Doing It Wrong](https://hbr.org/2013/09/building-a-minimum-viable-prod) - Harvard Business Review - Explains how founders should design experiments that validate or invalidate specific business-model assumptions, illustrating this chapter's distinction between an untested assumption and validated learning.

8. [Startup Business Ideas: 4 Steps to Identify the Right One](https://www.sba.gov/blog/startup-business-ideas-4-steps-identify-right-one) - U.S. Small Business Administration - A four-step framework for turning a passion into a tested idea, ending in real-world testing with customers, mirroring this chapter's path from unrefined concept to validated action.

9. [How to Come Up with an Innovative Business Idea](https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/how-to-come-up-with-a-business-idea) - Harvard Business School Online - Outlines four practical approaches for generating a business idea rooted in genuine customer needs, helping readers move an unrefined concept toward a testable business idea.

10. [Startup School](https://www.startupschool.org/) - Y Combinator - A free, self-paced course covering early-stage venture fundamentals, including MVP planning and user acquisition, giving readers a practical extension of this chapter's early-stage venture concepts.
