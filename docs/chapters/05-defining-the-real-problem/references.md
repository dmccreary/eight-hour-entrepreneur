# References: Defining the Real Problem

1. [Five whys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_whys) - Wikipedia - Explains the iterative "why" questioning technique for finding root causes, the exact method this chapter uses to trace a customer's first complaint down to the underlying condition actually producing it.

2. [Root-cause analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_cause_analysis) - Wikipedia - Covers systematic methods for distinguishing a fault's fundamental cause from its symptoms, supporting this chapter's discipline of not stopping at the first explanation a customer offers.

3. [Problem statement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_statement) - Wikipedia - Describes how a problem statement identifies a gap and facilitates communication of an issue, directly relevant to this chapter's guidance on writing the finished problem statement that goes in the canvas's Problem block.

4. The Mom Test - Rob Fitzpatrick - Self-published - Fitzpatrick pioneered a question format for customer conversations that avoids leading questions and false-positive validation, the exact discipline this chapter needs to tell a real problem from a perceived one.

5. Competing Against Luck - Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, and David S. Duncan - Harper Business - Christensen pioneered Jobs to Be Done theory, reframing a problem around the functional task a customer is trying to accomplish, exactly the reframing this chapter uses to look past a stated symptom.

6. [Know Your Customers' "Jobs to Be Done"](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done) - Harvard Business Review - Explains how focusing on the underlying job a customer is hiring a product to do produces more predictable innovation, reinforcing this chapter's distinction between a symptom and the job to be done beneath it.

7. [The Customer Profile](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-customer-profile) - Strategyzer - Breaks a customer down into jobs, pains, and gains, a practical tool for the same root-cause and unmet-need thinking this chapter applies to move past a customer's first-stated complaint.

8. [Validate Customer Demand](https://innovationlabs.harvard.edu/how-to/validate-demand) - Harvard Innovation Labs - Outlines three validation questions, including budget and priority, and explicitly recommends The Mom Test's non-leading interview approach, directly reinforcing this chapter's willingness-to-solve and problem-validation concepts.

9. [Every Successful Business Solves a Pain Point — Does Yours?](https://www.entrepreneur.com/starting-a-business/every-business-needs-to-solve-a-pain-point-does-yours/487290) - Entrepreneur.com - Argues that products fail without a genuine, validated pain point behind them and recommends manual testing before building, reinforcing this chapter's distinction between a real problem and a merely perceived one.

10. [Five Whys Technique in User Research](https://dovetail.com/design-thinking/five-whys/) - Dovetail - A practical walkthrough of applying the Five Whys to a real product problem, showing how repeated "why" questions can reveal an audience mismatch rather than a surface-level defect, mirroring this chapter's root-cause drill-down.
