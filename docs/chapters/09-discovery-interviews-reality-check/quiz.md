#### 1. What is the primary purpose of a discovery interview, according to this chapter?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. To learn about a prospective customer's problem, current behavior, and unmet needs — not to pitch, describe, or sell a solution
2. To close a sale with a prospective customer as quickly as possible
3. To measure how many customers would click "buy" on a landing page
4. To gather quantitative data on how many customers experience a problem
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. A discovery interview is a structured conversation with a real prospective customer, designed specifically to learn about their problem, current behavior, and unmet needs — not to pitch, describe, or sell them anything. The moment a founder starts selling, the interview stops producing trustworthy data.

#### 4. Which of the following is an open-ended question, using the chapter's own example?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>

#### 5. Which of the following is a leading question, using the chapter's own example?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. "Wouldn't it be great if grooming came to your house?"
2. "Tell me about the last time this was a problem for you."
3. "Walk me through what you currently do when your dog needs a bath."
4. "What's the most frustrating part of your current routine, if anything?"
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. A leading question is worded in a way that suggests the "correct" or desired answer, nudging the customer toward agreement rather than honesty. "Wouldn't it be great if..." practically demands a yes, unlike the other three genuinely open-ended options.
