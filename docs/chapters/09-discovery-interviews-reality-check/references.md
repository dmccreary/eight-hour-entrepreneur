# References: Discovery Interviews & the Reality Check

1. [Customer Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_development) - Wikipedia - Covers Steve Blank's methodology of testing startup hypotheses through direct customer conversation rather than assumption, the discipline this entire chapter teaches founders to execute well.
2. [Leading Question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading_question) - Wikipedia - Explains how a question's wording can suggest its own desired answer, the exact failure mode this chapter's open-ended-versus-leading-question distinction is designed to prevent.
3. [Qualitative Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualitative_research) - Wikipedia - Describes research that captures opinions and motivations in people's own words rather than counts, the category of evidence a discovery interview is built to produce.
4. The Mom Test - Rob Fitzpatrick - Robfitz Ltd - Credited for the specific technique of anchoring interview questions in a customer's past behavior instead of future hypotheticals, the direct source of this chapter's asking-not-selling and open-ended-question discipline.
5. The Startup Owner's Manual - Steve Blank, Bob Dorf - K&S Ranch - Credited for the Customer Development process and the "get out of the building" discipline, the origin of the discovery interview practice this chapter teaches step by step.
6. [Avoid Leading Questions to Get Better Insights from Participants](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/leading-questions/) - Nielsen Norman Group - Explains how leading questions bias research participants into confirming a researcher's assumptions, reinforcing this chapter's warning against interview bias.
7. [How to Talk to Users](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Iq-how-to-talk-to-users) - Y Combinator Startup Library - A practitioner's guide to running productive user conversations and interpreting the feedback gathered, directly supporting this chapter's interview script and debrief practices.
8. [Getting Out of the Building: Customer Development](https://www.entrepreneurship.org/learning-paths/the-lean-approach/getting-out-of-the-building-customer-development) - Kauffman Founders School - Frames customer development as testing every business hypothesis through direct outside conversation rather than in-house assumption, echoing this chapter's reality check exercise.
9. [The Surprising Power of Questions](https://hbr.org/2018/05/the-surprising-power-of-questions) - Harvard Business Review - Draws on behavioral research to show how question framing and attentive follow-up build trust and surface real information, informing this chapter's guidance on writing and asking interview questions.
10. [3 Methods for Identifying Customer Needs](https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/methods-for-identifying-customer-needs) - Harvard Business School Online - Recommends one-on-one interviews with current, former, and prospective customers as a core method for uncovering unmet needs, directly paralleling this chapter's discovery interview practice.
