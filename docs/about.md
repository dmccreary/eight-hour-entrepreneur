# About This Book

## Why This Intelligent Textbook

More people are trying to start a business than ever before, but many new
ventures close before they ever find customers who will pay. One of the most
common reasons is not a lack of effort. It is spending months and savings
building something before checking whether anyone wants it.

**In the United States:**

- Americans filed a record **5.67 million** applications to start new
  businesses in 2025[^1]
- Roughly **1 in 5** new business establishments closes within its first
  year, about **half** are gone within five years, and only about **1 in 3**
  is still open after ten years[^2]
- The country has **36.2 million** small businesses employing **45.9%** of
  private-sector workers, and **29.8 million** of them have no employees
  other than the owner[^3]
- **18.5%** of U.S. adults are starting or running a new business, but only
  **5.5%** own an established one, and **55%** of founders rely on personal
  savings to get started (2025 survey)[^4]
- The National Science Foundation has trained more than **2,500** research
  teams in customer discovery through its I-Corps program, and nearly
  **1,400** of those teams have gone on to launch startups[^5]

**Around the world and in the research:**

- Fear of failure keeps roughly **2 in 5 adults** worldwide from starting a
  business, according to a 2025 survey of more than 160,000 people in 53
  economies[^6]
- In an analysis of **431** venture-backed startups that shut down since
  2023, **43%** cited poor product-market fit, and **70%** ran out of capital
  — usually the final blow rather than the root cause[^7]
- In a randomized controlled trial with **116** startups, founders trained to
  treat their business ideas as hypotheses to test performed better and were
  more likely to pivot than founders who were not[^8]

Behind each of these numbers is someone like the founders in your cohort: a
person with a real idea, limited savings, and no time to spend a year
building the wrong thing. This book exists to help them find out quickly,
and cheaply, whether their idea deserves that year.

This book takes a different approach from a traditional business-plan
course. It is built on a **learning graph of 300 interconnected concepts**
organized into 14 categories, and concepts are introduced only after the
ideas they depend on have been covered. Instead of a multi-year plan,
founders complete all 11 blocks of the **CO.STARTERS Canvas**, interview real
customers during **Field Discovery Windows** between sessions, and run
lean validation tests — service before product, manual before automated,
pre-selling before building — the same test-your-hypotheses discipline the
research above rewards. **Interactive MicroSims** let founders practice each
method in the browser before trying it on real customers. The entire
textbook is written in plain language for people with no business background,
and it is **open source and free** — no paywalls, no access codes, no
expensive annual editions.

## How to Use This Book

*The Eight-Hour Entrepreneur* is the companion textbook for a four-week
virtual learning lab: four 2-hour live sessions separated by 7-day Field
Discovery Windows. You can read it alongside a live cohort or work through it
on your own. Each chapter builds on the ones before it, so reading in order
is recommended. The book includes:

- **17 Chapters** that walk through all 11 blocks of the CO.STARTERS Canvas —
  from customer discovery and problem definition, through alternatives,
  value propositions, and lean validation tests, to messaging, distribution,
  pricing, cost planning, a 30-day launch plan, and a 2-minute pitch
- **20 Interactive MicroSims** — browser-based tools such as a break-even
  calculator, a competitive landscape map, and a two-minute pitch timer that
  let you practice each method before you use it with real customers. Some
  are still being built; the colored dot beside each MicroSim in the
  navigation shows its current status.
- **Scout the Fox**, a learning guide who shows up at key moments with tips,
  warnings about common founder mistakes, and a push to go test your
  assumptions
- **A Learning Graph** of 300 concepts showing how every idea in the course
  depends on the ones before it
- **Search** available from any page using the search bar

The [Learning Graph](learning-graph/index.md) visualizes how concepts connect
across chapters. If you want to jump to a specific topic or check what you
need to know first, start there. The [Course Description](course-description.md)
lists the full learning outcomes.

## About the Author

![Valerie Lockhart](img/valerie-lockhart-headshot.jpg){ width="180px" align="right" }

Valerie Lockhart runs [MTN.org](https://mtn.org) and has spent the last 30
years providing executive management and operational functions for
nonprofits, small businesses, and corporations, including legal compliance
and human resources. MTN.org also hosts the online community hub that keeps
each cohort of this course connected between live sessions.

Valerie draws on more than 25 years of experience in photography,
videography, web development, search engine optimization (SEO), content
creation and management, graphic design, and writing and editing to produce
engaging training and educational resources for people who want to create
with technology. Her expertise spans information and communication
technology (ICT), media creation, and the intersection of art and technology.

She is passionate about access, individual rights, and freedom of
information and expression, and she works to foster a culture of inclusion,
collaboration, and innovation in the technology industry. That commitment is
why this book is open and free: every founder who arrives with an idea should
have the tools to test it, whatever their background or budget.

materials, or other publications, please use one of the following citation
formats.

**APA (7th edition)**

**Chicago (17th edition)**

Lockhart, Valerie. 2026. *The Eight-Hour Entrepreneur*. https://dmccreary.github.io/eight-hour-entrepreneur/.
