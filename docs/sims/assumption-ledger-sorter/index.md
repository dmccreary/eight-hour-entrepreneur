## About This MicroSim

Every business idea starts out built entirely from assumptions. The skill this MicroSim
practices is telling the difference between a statement someone has actually **tested**
and one that only **sounds** true.

Learners work through six cards from Jordan's ledger, the running list of beliefs and
findings behind Jordan's mobile dog-grooming idea. For each card they choose
**Assumption** or **Validated Learning**. The feedback is immediate:

- A correct answer flashes **green**; an incorrect answer flashes **amber**.
- The correct category is stamped on the card.
- A one-sentence explanation says why, so a wrong answer still teaches something.

Some cards are deliberately tricky. "2 of 5 neighbors said the price was too high" is
validated learning even though it is bad news, and "A mobile service is obviously more
convenient" is an assumption even though it sounds reasonable.

## How to Use

1. Read the statement on the card.
2. Click **Assumption** or **Validated Learning**.
3. Read the feedback, then click **Next Card**.
4. After card 6, click **See Results** for your score and a review of any cards you missed.
5. Click **Try Again** to sort the same six cards in a new, shuffled order.

The progress bar across the top turns green or amber for each card as you go. Nothing is
saved; the score lasts only for the current session.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Adult early-stage entrepreneurs and aspiring founders in a live virtual cohort.

### Learning Objective
Given short statements from Jordan's dog-grooming-van idea, participants will classify each
one as an Assumption (untested) or Validated Learning (tested), applying the chapter's
definitions to concrete cases.

**Bloom Level:** Apply (L3). **Bloom Verb:** classify.

### Duration
5-10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Chapter 1 reading on assumptions and validated learning

### Activities

1. **Solo sort (4 min):** Each participant sorts all six cards and notes their score.
2. **Debrief the tricky cards (3 min):** As a group, discuss cards 4 and 5. Why is bad news
   still validated learning? Why does "obviously" signal an untested belief?
3. **Sort your own ledger (3 min):** Each participant writes three statements about their
   own idea and labels each one, asking "how do I know this?" for every line.

### Assessment
- Did the participant classify at least 5 of 6 cards correctly on a second attempt?
- Can the participant explain why a customer's "no" counts as validated learning?
- Can the participant rewrite one of their own assumptions as a test they could run this week?
