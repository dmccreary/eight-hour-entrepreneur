## About This MicroSim

Jordan's finished value proposition follows a pattern with four parts:

> [Target Customer] gets [Core Benefit], from [Founder Advantage], unlike [Main Alternative].

Each part answers one question a stranger would ask:

| Part | Question it answers | Jordan's wording | Priya's wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target Customer | Who is it for? | busy dog owners without easy groomer access | dual-income parents on rotating shifts |
| Core Benefit | What do they get? | grooming without losing an afternoon | a real dinner without any advance planning |
| Founder Advantage | Why you? | someone they already trust in the neighborhood | someone who understands unpredictable schedules firsthand |
| Main Alternative | Compared with what? | the booked-solid groomer across town | another night of takeout |

This MicroSim has one dropdown for each part. Every dropdown offers Jordan's wording,
Priya's wording, and **Write your own**, which opens a text box beside the dropdown.
The preview card rebuilds the sentence on every change, as you type, and wraps it to fit
the screen. Each part keeps its own color, and a missing part stays as a bracketed
placeholder. The verb agrees with the customer: "parents get" but "a nurse gets".

Below the preview, the sentence gets formative feedback:

- **Parts filled in:** a counter shows how many of the four parts are done.
- **Read time:** once all four parts are in, a word count and an estimated read time at
  about 4 words per second. Green means 8 seconds or less, amber means 9-10 seconds, and
  dark orange means over the ten-second goal from the chapter.
- **One message:** the most important next step. In priority order, the message flags
  a missing part, parts mixed from two founders, generic words that any competitor
  could claim (such as "affordable" or "high quality"), and a sentence that is too long
  to read in ten seconds. A complete sentence gets a next step instead.

Both founders' versions come in at about 7 seconds (27 and 25 words).

On a phone-width screen each role label moves above its dropdown and the button labels
shorten.

## How to Use

1. Click **Load Jordan's Version** and read the finished sentence. Match each colored
   part to its dropdown.
2. Click **Load Priya's Version** and compare. The template is the same; every part
   changes.
3. Change one dropdown back to Jordan's wording. Read the warning about mixing two
   ventures.
4. Click **Clear**, then choose **Write your own** in each dropdown and type the four
   parts for your own idea. Watch the sentence, word count, and read time update as you
   type.
5. Revise until the message box turns green, then copy your sentence out for the
   five-second test.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience

Adult early-stage entrepreneurs and aspiring founders in a live virtual cohort.

### Learning Objective

Participants will compose a one-sentence value proposition for their own venture by
choosing or writing a target customer, core benefit, founder advantage, and main
alternative, and revise it until it reads clearly in under ten seconds.

**Bloom Level:** Create (L6). **Bloom Verb:** compose.

### Duration

15-20 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Chapter 8: Value Proposition: Benefit & Advantage (the value statement)
- Chapter 12 reading through "The One-Sentence Value Proposition"

### Activities

1. **Take apart a finished example (3 min):** The facilitator shares the screen and
   loads Jordan's version. Participants name which question each colored part answers.
2. **Break it on purpose (2 min):** The facilitator swaps one part to Priya's wording.
   The group explains in the chat why the mixed sentence no longer describes one
   business.
3. **Compose your own (7 min):** Each participant chooses Write your own in all four
   dropdowns and types the parts for their own venture, revising until the message box
   turns green and the read time is under ten seconds.
4. **Five-second swap (5 min):** In breakout pairs, each partner shows their sentence for
   five seconds, hides it, and asks the other to say back what the business offers.
   Partners then cut any word the listener didn't need.

### Assessment

- Does the participant's sentence fill all four parts with specific language, not
  wording any competitor could claim?
- Is the sentence 32 words or fewer, so it reads in about 8 seconds or less?
- Can a partner repeat back the customer, the benefit, and the alternative after
  five seconds?
- Can the participant explain why a sentence mixing two ventures' parts fails even
  though it is grammatical?

   Wikipedia - The single most compelling point of difference, which the Founder

   spoken form of a value proposition that grows out of the one-sentence version.
4. [Words per minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_per_minute) - Wikipedia -
   Typical adult reading speeds, the basis for the read-time estimate of about 4 words
   per second.
5. *Value Proposition Design* by Alexander Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur, Greg Bernarda, and
   Alan Smith (2014) - A practical guide to matching a product's value to a specific
   customer's jobs, pains, and gains.
