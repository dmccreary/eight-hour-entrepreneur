## About This MicroSim

A value proposition answers two separate questions: why the customer wants the solution
(the **Benefit**) and why this founder is the one to deliver it (the **Advantage**). Most
weak pitches answer only one of them, and founders often mix the two up in their own
writing.

Learners work through six statements from Jordan's mobile dog-grooming pitch and choose
**Benefit** or **Advantage** for each. The feedback is immediate:

- A correct answer flashes **green**; an incorrect answer flashes **amber**.
- The correct category is stamped on the card.
- A one-sentence explanation says why, so a wrong answer still teaches something.

Two cards are deliberately tricky. "Affordable, convenient grooming that comes to you"
sounds like a claim about the business, but it describes value the customer receives, so
it is a benefit. "I can respond same-day" helps the customer, but it rests on a fact about
the founder (Jordan lives in the neighborhood), so it is an advantage.

## How to Use

1. Read the statement on the card.
2. Click **Benefit** or **Advantage**.
3. Read the feedback, then click **Next Card**.
4. After card 6, click **See Results** for your score and a review of all six statements.
5. Click **Try Again** to sort the same six cards in a new, shuffled order.

The progress bar across the top turns green or amber for each card as you go. The stack
behind the current card shrinks as cards are dealt. Nothing is saved; the score lasts only
for the current session.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience

Adult early-stage entrepreneurs and aspiring founders in a live virtual cohort.

### Learning Objective

Given short value-proposition statements, participants will classify each one as a
customer benefit statement (why the customer wants it) or a founder advantage statement
(why this founder can deliver it), applying the chapter's definitions.

**Bloom Level:** Apply (L3). **Bloom Verb:** classify.

### Duration

5-10 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Chapter 8 reading on customer benefit, functional and emotional benefits, founder
  advantage, domain expertise, and founder credibility

### Activities

1. **Solo sort (4 min):** Each participant sorts all six cards and notes their score.
2. **Debrief the tricky cards (3 min):** As a group, discuss the "affordable, convenient"
   card and the "same-day" card. Which words make each one sound like the other category?
   Who is the sentence actually about?
3. **Sort your own pitch (3 min):** Each participant writes one benefit sentence and one
   advantage sentence for their own idea, then trades with a partner who labels them
   without seeing the answers.

### Assessment

- Did the participant classify at least 5 of 6 cards correctly on a second, shuffled attempt?
- Can the participant explain why "affordable, convenient grooming" is a benefit even
  though it sounds like a pitch about the business?
- Can the participant name which ingredient of founder advantage (domain expertise or
  founder credibility) a given advantage statement relies on?
