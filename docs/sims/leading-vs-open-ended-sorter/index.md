## About This MicroSim

How a question is worded decides whether a discovery interview produces evidence or just
polite agreement. An **open-ended question** asks the customer to supply the content in
their own words. A **leading question** supplies the content itself and only asks the
customer to agree.

Learners work through six cards, each a question Jordan might ask a dog owner about
grooming, and choose **Open-Ended** or **Leading**. The feedback is immediate:

- A correct answer flashes **green**; an incorrect answer flashes **amber**.
- The correct category is stamped on the card.
- A one-sentence explanation says why, so a wrong answer still teaches something.
- Every leading question gets an **open-ended rewrite** on the card, showing how to ask
  the same thing without suggesting the answer.

Two cards are deliberately tricky. "Tell me about the last time getting your dog groomed
was a hassle" mentions a problem, yet it is still open-ended because the customer tells
the story. "This would save you so much time, right?" is a statement with "right?" added
at the end, and that tag is what makes it leading.

## How to Use

1. Read the interview question on the card.
2. Click **Open-Ended** or **Leading**.
3. Read the feedback. For a leading question, read the open-ended rewrite on the card.
4. Click **Next Card**. After card 6, click **See Results** for your score and a review of
   all six questions.
5. Click **Try Again** to sort the same six cards in a new, shuffled order.

The progress bar across the top turns green or amber for each card as you go. The stack
behind the current card shrinks as cards are dealt. Nothing is saved; the score lasts only
for the current session.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience

Adult early-stage entrepreneurs and aspiring founders in a live virtual cohort.

### Learning Objective

Given sample discovery-interview questions, participants will classify each one as
open-ended or leading, applying the chapter's unbiased interview design principles.

**Bloom Level:** Apply (L3). **Bloom Verb:** classify.

### Duration

10 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Chapter 9 reading on discovery interviews, especially the section on asking, not selling

### Activities

1. **Solo sort (3 min):** Each participant sorts all six cards and notes their score.
2. **Debrief the tricky cards (3 min):** As a group, discuss the "hassle" card and the
   "right?" card. What makes the first one open-ended even though it names a problem?
   Why does adding "right?" turn a statement into a leading question?
3. **Rewrite your own script (4 min):** Each participant writes three questions for their
   own discovery interviews, runs the stranger test on each one (could a stranger answer
   "no" without feeling awkward?), and rewrites any that fail as open-ended questions.

### Assessment

- Did the participant classify at least 5 of 6 cards correctly on a second, shuffled attempt?
- Can the participant explain the difference in terms of who supplies the content: the
  customer or the question?
- Can the participant rewrite a leading question from their own script as an open-ended
  question about past behavior?
