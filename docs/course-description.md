# Course Description: The Eight-Hour Entrepreneur

**Title:** The Eight-Hour Entrepreneur

**Target Audience:** Adult professional development — early-stage entrepreneurs and aspiring founders participating in a live virtual cohort.

**Prerequisites:** None. Participants should arrive with an unrefined business idea or concept they want to test; no prior business, startup, or entrepreneurship experience is required.

## Course Overview

The Eight-Hour Entrepreneur is an intensive, virtual "learning lab" designed to take early-stage entrepreneurs from an unrefined concept to a validated, launch-ready venture in 8 total hours, delivered as four 2-hour virtual sessions over four weeks. Rather than running as a traditional 2-day CO.STARTERS Bootcamp, this virtual adaptation inserts 7-day gaps between weekly sessions as "Field Discovery Windows" — intervals that force participants out of the "idea trap" and into testing their assumptions against real-world customer friction.

The course operationalize a core philosophy — Clarity → Validation → Action → Launch — and is centered on all 11 building blocks of the CO.STARTERS Canvas (Customer, Problem, Solution, Alternatives, Benefit, Advantage, Message, Distribution, Revenue, Startup Needs, Costs). Delivery combines a virtual live cohort (video conferencing with breakout rooms), collaborative digital canvas whiteboards (Google Slides), and an asynchronous community hub (Slack, provided by MTN.org).

By replacing multi-year theoretical business plans with an immediate, milestone-driven 30-day launch roadmap backed by peer accountability, the course matters because it compresses the founder journey from idea to evidence-based action, teaching lean, low-capital validation methods that reduce the risk of building something nobody wants.

## Main Topics Covered

1. Course philosophy and the Clarity → Validation → Action → Launch framework
2. The CO.STARTERS Canvas and its 11 building blocks
3. Customer identification and target-customer definition
4. Problem definition and pain-point discovery
5. Solution framing and the initial offer
6. Customer discovery interviews and the "Reality Check" pressure test
7. Competitive alternatives and market landscape analysis
8. Benefit articulation — why the customer wants the solution
9. Competitive advantage and founder differentiation
10. Lean validation testing (service before product, manual before automated, pre-selling before building)
11. Messaging and value-proposition writing
12. Distribution channels
13. Revenue models and pricing
14. Startup needs (one-time resources) versus ongoing costs
15. 30-day launch planning and weekly milestone setting
16. Pitching and peer accountability networks

## Topics Not Covered

- Formal legal structuring and business incorporation
- Writing a traditional, full-length, multi-year business plan
- Fundraising, investment pitching, and equity/cap-table mechanics
- Bookkeeping, accounting systems, and tax filing
- Hiring, HR policy, and team management
- Product engineering, software development, or manufacturing execution
- Paid-advertising campaign management and digital-marketing tooling
- Scaling operations, franchising, or multi-location growth beyond initial launch

## Learning Outcomes

After completing this course, students will be able to:

### Remember
*Retrieving, recognizing, and recalling relevant knowledge from long-term memory.*

- Recall the 11 building blocks of the CO.STARTERS Canvas: Customer, Problem, Solution, Alternatives, Benefit, Advantage, Message, Distribution, Revenue, Startup Needs, and Costs.
- List the four phases of the course philosophy: Clarity, Validation, Action, Launch.
- Identify the components of a minimum testable iteration: service before product, manual before automated, pre-selling before building.
- Recall the purpose of a Field Discovery Window between sessions.

### Understand
*Constructing meaning from instructional messages, including oral, written, and graphic communication.*

- Explain why customer discovery interviews should ask questions rather than sell.
- Explain the purpose of the Reality Check pressure test in distinguishing real pain points from assumed ones.
- Describe how the Field Discovery Window operationalize real-world validation between sessions.
- Explain the relationship between the Alternatives, Benefit, and Advantage canvas blocks.

### Apply
*Carrying out or using a procedure in a given situation.*

- Conduct structured discovery interviews with at least three real prospective customers.
- Complete each of the 11 CO.STARTERS Canvas blocks for an original venture concept.
- Design and execute a lean validation test, such as a pre-sale offer or manual service delivery.
- Draft a one-sentence value proposition and high-conversion messaging copy.

### Analyze
*Breaking material into constituent parts and determining how the parts relate to one another and to an overall structure or purpose.*

- Analyze customer-interview findings to identify surprises and dismantle weak assumptions.
- Differentiate one-time Startup Needs from ongoing Costs for a specific venture.
- Evaluate lean-test results to determine whether an assumption was validated (did anyone say yes, did anyone pay).
- Compare a venture's offering against existing alternatives and competitors to identify genuine differentiation.

### Evaluate
*Making judgments based on criteria and standards through checking and critiquing.*

- Judge whether a business concept has reached sufficient validation to proceed toward launch.
- Critique a peer's business concept and provide constructive, evidence-based pressure-test feedback.
- Assess pricing and distribution-channel choices against a venture's revenue goals.
- Decide whether to persevere, pivot, or kill a concept based on real-world evidence gathered during the course.

### Create
*Putting elements together to form a coherent or functional whole; reorganizing elements into a new pattern or structure.*

- Build a complete CO.STARTERS Canvas for an original venture concept.
- Develop a 30-day launch plan defining what to sell, who to sell to, how to reach them, and weekly targets.
- Produce a polished 2-minute pitch communicating the validated concept to peers.
- Design an initial go-to-market messaging package combining the value proposition and sales copy. (Capstone: present the completed canvas, 30-day launch plan, and pitch in the Week 4 Mini Pitch + Commitments session.)
